### 7 Figure Copy - Sean Vosler (Dokument) (Course C) [L0196, 2020+] (continued)

- Bob Serling Power Copywriting Formula, 36 steps for long-form sales pages: quality product; customer profile; credibility; offer; exhaustive research; rest to let ideas percolate; list features, facts and figures; list every benefit; irresistible offer; great guarantee; attention-grabbing headline; colour to highlight key points; limit graphics; no-holds-barred opener delivering on the headline; eliminate early objections; enticing crossheads; make them feel the pain deeply; eliminate the pain; establish credibility; lock it in with an "insider benefit"; unquestionable proof; readable chunks; list benefits; summarise key benefits; list features; go above and beyond with the package; state the price; call to action; piggy-back offer to boost average order value; minimise risk; close summarising major benefits; add a PS; make it easy to buy; eliminate distracting links; let the copy rest; revise for maximum impact. (doc appendix p.2)
- Craig Clemens sales letter formula: headline; subhead; questions that show you understand and push buttons; powerful analogy or story; "it's not your fault and there's hope"; give away content; proof; what you are offering and what's in it; bullets/benefits; who needs it and who doesn't; what makes it different; wrap-up; price; guarantee; close; signature; PS; testimonials. Star-Story-Solution: introduce the star, tell their story, present the solution that helped them. Seven-Step formula (Bob Stone): key benefit; explore and build it; what readers get specifically; proof; risks of not acting; summary of benefits; CTA. Nine-Point formula: headline; inspiring lede; define the product; success story; testimonials and endorsements; special features or value-adds; value proposition; move to action with specifics and urgency; postscript. 12-Step sales letter: attention; problem; solution; credentials; benefits; social proof; offer; scarcity; guarantee; CTA; warning; close with a reminder. (doc appendix p.2-3)
- Perry Belcher 21-part sales letter (first 10 steps listed): 1. call out the audience; 2. big-promise headline; 3. back it with a quick explanation; 4. identify the core or most painful problem; 5. provide the solution; 6. show the pain and cost of developing the solution; 7. explain ease of use; 8. show speed to results; 9. future pace; 10. show credentials. HELLYEAH: Holler for attention; Empathise (share your pains); Lambast what caused the problem; Legwork (prove it); Yes, you have the solution; Educate why yours is best; Action; Handle lingering objections. P.A.S.T.O.R: Person/problem/pain; Amplify consequences; Story/solution/system; Testimonials; Offer (spend 80% of the offer section on transformation, Ray Edwards); Response. (doc appendix p.3-4)
- VSL formulas: Jim Edwards method: 1. shocking statement; 2. problem and why it's a big deal; 3. agitate; 4. agitate further to a deep emotional level; 5. introduce solution; 6. credibility; 7. proof; 8. biggest things they get (features and benefits); 9. specific reasons to act now; 10. close by asking for the purchase and reinforcing benefits. Common 12-step VSL template: attention-grabbing greeting; identify problem and promise to solve it; establish scarcity; agitate; your solution as THE solution; features and benefits; first CTA (desire); credentials/proof/results/testimonials; guarantee; second CTA (logic); warn of FOMO with deadline and scarcity; final CTA (FOMO). (doc appendix p.4)

### Audience and Creatives Process (Dokument) (Course C) [L0258, undated]

- Competitor UGC breakdown worked example (Leaf & Twig razor): concept or angle is testimonial, unboxing, advertorial or problem-solution. Hook in the first 3 seconds: "I just got the Leaf & Twig Razor". Problems or desires: no leg rashes, silky smooth skin, keeps throwing away plastic razors (not sustainable), razors rust. Benefits: less plastic, saves money long-term, recycling program. Features: stainless steel, metal razor with pivoting head. Pain point: "strawberry legs" after shaving. Objection: "definitely rusts", answered by "X technology makes it rust-free". Character: female, 25 to 35. Scenery: normal apartment, living room, dining table. Slang: none.
- Transformation chain example: no rust means a clean shave, which means soft and smooth skin, which means you'll look and feel beautiful. The underlying motivations are belonging, connection and significance.
- Comparative framework ("we vs the others") steps: split screen, then your USP vs the industry (what you do better, what others lack), then CTA.
- Problem/Agitate/Solve spreadsheet: if the product doesn't solve a problem, intensify or accentuate the desired outcome instead.
- Good attention + weak interest + good desire: the wrong target group may be seeing the ad.
