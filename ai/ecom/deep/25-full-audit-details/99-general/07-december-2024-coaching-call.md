### DotCom Secrets by Russell Brunson (Dokument) (Course C) [L0523, 2015+]

#### Secrets 1 to 8
- Secret #1: the consulting price is stated as $25,000 per day; the author tells readers to treat the four-question exercise as if they had paid that.
- Secret #1 avatars: two named avatars. "Julie": successful and driven, has a message to share, values personal growth over money, business already at least five figures a year. "Mike": former athlete, has changed someone's life in a small way and wants to help more, values growth over money, business already at least five figures a year. Find a photo for each by searching Google images with the listed traits.
- Secret #6 storylines (six types): Loss and Redemption template ("I had everything ... then ___ happened ... blessing in disguise ... now I ___"); Before and After ("First I was ___. Now I'm ___", can be told with pictures alone, works in any market); Amazing Discovery ("wait til you hear about this amazing new thing", huge for selling webinars and teleseminars); Secret Telling ("I've got a secret ... if you want to find out what it is, you need to do ___", the author's favourite, the lure of secrets draws the reader up the ladder).
- Secret #8: both example Seinfeld emails (the golf-product applicant story and the Jiu Jitsu tournament story) each made over $100,000 when sent to the list; the second ended with the claim of six figures in sales in a week without launches, affiliates or the author in the office. Emails can be dictated on a phone and sent to an assistant to transcribe; writing prompts are offered as a download.
#### Secrets 10 to 12
- Secret #10 phase 4: Dan Kennedy's rule "a buyer is a buyer is a buyer": whoever buys once keeps buying while you keep giving value, so after the opt-in send people straight to a buy page, not a day or a week later.
- Secret #10 phase 5 example: a bowling loss made the author buy books, videos and a new ball at once; the "hyperactive" window closed a few days later, which is why a chain of upsells and downsells must be ready immediately.
- Secret #10 cold-traffic bridge example: cold visitors must be spoken to in simple language ("people" not "traffic", "nerve pain" not "neuropathy"). The ListHacking funnel started with "Who wants a free money-making website?", then a bridge page explained what traffic and lists are before the membership offer.
- Secret #11 hyperactive blocks: order-form bumps are two lines of text plus a checkbox and get up to 40% of buyers to add $37 or more at the point of sale.
- Secret #11: one-time offers: often two to three separate offers after the purchase, as long as each adds value to the first.
- Secret #11: downsales: up to 20% of people who say no to the one-time offer say yes to a payment-plan version of it.
- Secret #11: affiliate recommendations are placed on the thank-you page after the upsell/downsell sequence ends.
- Secret #11: the author's neuropathy supplement did not work as free-plus-shipping ("the numbers never quite worked out") but worked as a straight sale.
- Secret #12: which funnel for which traffic: cold traffic starts at the front with Free-Plus-Shipping or Self-Liquidating-Offer funnels; as they warm up through the communication funnel, mid-ladder funnels are used (Perfect Webinar, Invisible Funnel, Product Launch); hot traffic goes to the backend, High-Ticket Two-Step, moved to phone or live event. The seven funnels start at the landing page (they do not cover temperature or the pre-frame); customers you age and ascend count as warm traffic; the higher up the ladder, the longer the script.
- Secret #12 big picture: controlled traffic goes to a squeeze page, uncontrolled traffic to the blog; every opt-in then gets the Soap Opera Sequence, then daily Seinfeld emails; right after opt-in the person sees the first frontend offer, then point-of-sale upsells; later emails sell other ladder products and continuity.
#### Secret 13: The Best Bait
- Ariely experiment: Lindt truffle at 26 cents vs Hershey's Kiss at 1 cent: about 40% chose each; when both prices dropped by one cent (Kiss free) 90% took the free Kiss. Dropping 2 cents to 1 cent, or free to minus 1 cent, changed nothing: "power in free".
- The One-Hundred-Visitor Test: 100 cold visitors to a $197 product page converted about 1% = $197 and 1 new customer. With a free-plus-shipping page (CD, DVD or book) first, 8% bought it; about 25% of those bought the $197 upsell: $394 per 100 visitors and 8 new buyers (almost double the money, seven times the customers). The free page needed almost no copy. Result for the author's company: from about $30,000 a year online to over seven figures in less than 18 months.
- Why the free offer should be physical: it allows the word "free", the shipping payment qualifies the buyer as a card-holder, and it allows a one-click upsell. A digital freebie loses the qualifying, a small paid digital product loses the power of "free".
- Order-form bump discovery: the $37 product tested against a free CD (shipping paid) gave about three times as many customers for the free CD but a lower average cart value, so revenue was about the same. Adding a small box after the card details and before the submit button, offering the $37 product (the text also says $47 once), made about 34% add it: almost the same front-end revenue with three times as many people entering the upsell flow. The bump is recommended on every order form, even without free-plus-shipping.
- Real example DotComSecrets Labs: the book "108 Proven Split Test Winners" was planned at $997 and given away for $9.95 shipping instead. Bump: Conversion Krusher template $37. Upsell 1: Instant Traffic Hacks $197. Upsell 2: Perfect Webinar $297. Upsell 3: High-Ticket Secrets $997. Average about $66 immediate revenue per free book given away.
- Adaptations: ecommerce (birdcages: CD "How to Teach Your Parrot to Talk"; custom suits: free cuff links), author or coach (put the best solution into a book/CD/DVD), network marketer, affiliate marketer (own CD, interview an expert), offline owner. The free item must be unique, sexy or fun, not boring general knowledge.
#### Section 4: funnels and scripts
- Funnel #1 Two-Step Free-Plus-Shipping: page one has a short video using the Who/What/Why/How script and asks "where should I ship this?" (step 1 shipping address), step 2 asks card details for shipping and handling; the first page must state that shipping is charged (otherwise unethical and upsets customers); capture the email on step 1 so you can follow up with those who skip step 2; add the bump on step 2; then one to three upsells/downsells. Step-one conversion is often higher than on an email squeeze page, probably because something physical has higher perceived value.
- Who/What/Why/How script (inexpensive offers): Who (very brief intro), What (the free DVD), Why (benefits), How (fill in the form, shipping address), plus The Catch ("there is no catch ... I'm doing this because ___, all I need is help with shipping and handling"), Urgency (limited copies or an end date), Guarantee (refund and keep the DVD), Recap.
- OTO script: video only three to five minutes, closes price points from $97 to $2,000 or more. Getting the second yes is "80% offer structure and 20% script". Rule 1: do not sell more of the same thing. Rule 2: do not sell a random product. Three structures: The Next Thing (favourite; e.g. after a juicing book offer weight training or cardio), Do It Faster (never say the first purchase will not work without it), Need Help? (leads to high-end help).
- OTO script order: confirm the initial decision and keep the loop open ("Wait! Your order is not yet complete" instead of "Congratulations, your order is complete", which closed the loop and hurt conversion) > "Smart, and here's why" > "3x 2x" question (three times the results twice as fast) > Exclusive (not for everyone, you proved you're an action taker) > Results, Fast, Speed ("in half the time") > The One Thing (not everything: the Perfect Webinar product has 24+ hours of video but the OTO sells only "The Stack") > Future pacing > CTA > Guarantee > Value stacking (pull the thing people want most out of the product as a free bonus) > Scarcity ("when you leave this page it's gone forever") > second CTA > Testimonial rush.
- Funnel #2 Self-Liquidating Offer: for a product priced $27 to $97 (the building-block section says $37 to $97), the goal is to break even on traffic before upsells; free-plus-shipping loses money on the front end and breaks even through upsells. Flow: squeeze page with a lead magnet that is shown on the next page > SLO page (video or long letter, Star-Story-Solution script) > bump > OTO sequence.
- Star-Story-Solution script (origin: a man who made $1 million in 23 months selling supplements, 10 years to frame each section): 44 pieces. Star: 1 pattern interrupt, 2 core-desire questions, 3 agitate past failures, 4 big promise / the One Thing, 5 introduce the star. Story: 6 high drama, 7 backstory wall (example: 10,000-person list x 1% x $5 = $5,000), 8 identify the problem, 9 epiphany or declaration of independence, 10 your path to the solution, 11 first sign of success, 12 conspiracy, 13 the big lie, 14 common enemy, 15 rapid growth, 16 case studies, 17 hidden benefits. Solution: 18 formal introduction, 19 pain and cost, 20 ease, 21 speed, 22 "so" benefits (three or four benefits each followed by "so ___"), 23 social proof, 24 make the offer, 25 build value, 26 float a fake price (must be justified and ethical), 27 emotional close "If all" (toward pleasure and away from pain), 28 reveal the real price, 29 guarantee with a crazy name, 30 inject scarcity, then 40 future pacing, 41 call to action, 42 post selling, 43 take-away selling, 44 close with reminder. (The source text jumps from 30 to 40.)
- Funnel #3 Continuity (David Frey: "if you don't have continuity, then you don't have a business"): use it as the second funnel after customers bond with the Attractive Character, not as the first, because waiting "dramatically increases our stick rate"; with a free or $1 trial use the Who/What/Why/How script, without trial the Star-Story-Solution script; order-form bumps with training on using the program; yearly or lifetime accounts are very effective upsells; after the Two-Step or SLO funnel and the Soap Opera Sequence, send a short email sequence promoting it.
- Funnel #4 Perfect Webinar: runs about 60 to 90 minutes; registration page with Who/What/Why/How video; confirmation page; automated webinar recorded once (two of the author's webinars each made over $1 million); the replay of "yesterday's" webinar available at once; separate email sequences for: all subscribers, those who stay to the end, those who leave early, no-shows; sequences stop when someone orders. Script: introduction about 5 minutes (big promise repeated as the "ruler", hook to end such as a secret transcript link for live viewers, command attention, qualify yourself, future pace). Content 50 to 60 minutes: one main idea in the form "How to ___ without ___", then three secrets that break a belief and rebuild it (example: "can sell high-ticket products without phone calls"; secret 1: more in one day than in a month of normal products; secret 2: a two-person mini call center closes for you; secret 3: only about 100 clicks a day needed). The Stack: at least 10 minutes, often 20+; transition with "Let me ask you a question"; stack slide re-lists the whole offer after each new component (Armand Morin's method); the author's closing rate rose from 5-10% of a room to consistently 20% or more; then "If all" statements, then reveal the real price. There are 16 closes shown in a separate video.
- Funnel #5 Invisible Funnel Webinar (from Daegan Smith): a premium 3 to 4 hour webinar; register with a credit card but nothing is billed; charge the next day unless the person emails before a deadline. First run: 550 registered with cards, 85% attended (about 30% is common on free webinars), 4 hours taught, about 10% cancelled, 90% billed $47 = about $23.5K, plus coaching applications worth six figures in the next 30 days. Used for weight loss, dating, speed reading and more, a few times a year.
- Magic Bullet registration script (2 to 3 minute video letter, can be written in under an hour): raise the question (YES-answers, toward pleasure or away from pain, ending "Wouldn't it be nice if ..."), introduction, point out frustrations (your own), the Magic Bullet "You'll get ___ by ___ or you pay nothing" (examples: girlfriend this weekend, lose seven pounds in a 3-day detox, double reading speed in four hours), proof story (Howard Berg speed-reading $100 challenge), "don't worry" risk reversal, try-before-you-buy (listen free, do nothing and the card is charged $100, or email before midnight), the close.
- Invisible webinar content structure: re-tell the hook; tell what they will learn; per content piece: name, parable, concept, real example; recap; tie to value; "Was it worth $__ to you?" (do nothing = billed; email by the deadline = not billed); transition to the coaching offer; pick a close (website, free application, paid application); simple three-step close What/Why/How; practise content aloud to budget the time.
- Funnel #6 Product Launch (Jeff Walker): four videos of 10 to 20 minutes, best for warm/hot traffic by email (own list or affiliates): Video 1 "Wow and How" (big promise, kill myths, others' results, hook to video 2); Video 2 "Transformational Education" (do it together, how I do it step by step, proof, hook); Video 3 "Ownership Experience" (future pace, how YOU do it, hint at the offer, hook); Video 4 "The Offer" (what I've got, what you'll do with it, what it'll do for you, what to do right now, "three clicks").
- Funnel #7 High-Ticket Three-Step Application: the company once called all buyers with 60 sales people, costing hundreds of thousands of dollars a month, then replaced it with this funnel: "as many sales with just two sales people as with sixty". Step 1 case-study video and "apply now"; step 2 application (pre-qualifies, pre-frames); step 3 homework page with three videos (short class, personal story, case studies) that also tells them one of three sales people will call and gives a phone number for hyperactive buyers.
- High-Ticket Two-Step script: the whole phone process takes 75 minutes to 2 hours; sold in a changed environment (phone/live event); two people, a setter and a closer (the owner should not sell personally). Set script: intro, questions about the emotional "why", "what's holding you back" (usually "I don't know how": e.g. "If you knew how to build a business in five hours a week, would you?"), blast (taste of the program), the pivotal question "If you could work one-on-one with ___, do you think you'd be successful? How come?" then stay silent, posture the closer as the expert (Program Director), probe finances (age, marital status, partner on the call, credit rating, debt, major card debt, combined credit extended minus debt = available credit, which should exceed the price; savings, home, retirement), goals at 6 and 12 months, four commitments (minimum weekly time; coachable; start today; learn to use OPM / other people's money such as credit cards as short-term leverage), two price levels and "how much are you comfortable investing today?", then hand-off with a goal-writing exercise. Close script: re-ask the why questions, repeat the commitments as yes/no (time, decision today, investment number, teachability), list what's included, final question "Why do you feel you are a good candidate?", then take the card (he asks to buy). Hire two commission-based sales people for sales of $25,000, $50,000 or $100,000.
#### Section 5 and conclusion
- ClickFunnels workflow: sketch the funnel on a whiteboard first (funnel type, quiz or squeeze, frontend price, bump, number of upsells/downsells, follow-up sequence, Attractive Character); then in the software: 1 pick funnel type, 2 pick a template for each step, 3 finalise and see the funnel on a dashboard, 4 edit copy, video, autoresponder integration and order forms; built in about an hour, which took expert teams two to four weeks; free two-week trial; the software has simple split testing.
- Conclusion: what to work on first: 1 decide who you want to serve, 2 create your bait quickly without seeking perfection, 3 work out the value ladder and create the missing offers, 4 build funnels one at a time; then spend one to two weeks implementing one secret, move to the next, and repeat. Re-draw the diagrams from memory a day later to test recall.
