### 5.1 Creating Urgency (Dokument) (Course A) [L0137, undated]

- Teacher's own stores show a 5-10% off coupon every day of the year; the 10% codes expire twice a week. (document)
- Most competitors do not promote discount codes at all, and when they do the code is almost never as high as 10%, so a visible 10% code makes the store "the obvious choice". (document)
- Best way to offer the discount is "for a limited time only"; the urgency forces the visitor to decide now rather than risk missing out. (document)

### Bonus-Angebote zur Conversion-Steigerung (Course A) [L0314, 2020-26]

- [01:27] Bonus offers are not cross-selling or upselling: the item, upgrade or warranty is given free as a bonus.
- [00:00] Bonus offers are not necessary in the beginning, but the goal is to eventually think about and test a bonus offer for every supplier you work with.
- [04:54] You may run different bonus offers for different suppliers, depending on what each supplier has available (bonus for supplier A comes from supplier A).
- [05:45-06:11] Product-page display wording: in big font in the description section, "Our free gift to you. With all purchases of [product] you'll receive a free [item]", with a photo of the item and "Retail value $40" (leash example, wholesale $10-15), "included with your order for free when you buy from [store].com".
- [10:09] Extended warranty handling: when placing the order with that supplier, simply add the extended warranty to the order (you pay the extra $25, customer gets it included).
- [11:57] Action task: make a list per supplier of possible bonuses, then test them by adding the free bonus offer into the description section of the product pages.

### Conversion-Optimierung für Black Friday: Checkouts, Rabatte und Landingpages (Course C) [L0387, 2022]

### Conversion Rate Optimization: Testing, Vertrauen, Technik (Course A) [L0390, 2019]

- [06:49] Worked example for relative mobile conversion rate: big brands typically have about 4% desktop vs 2% mobile (= the 53% average), which "is not bad" because mobile visitors are earlier in the funnel.
- [31:45-32:35] Trust through professionalism and service: "relational capital" (how much you invest in customer relationships, e.g. calling the customer after the purchase to make sure they are happy) grows "reputational capital" so customers recommend the business; quick tricks are only a short-term solution for a long-term problem.
- [38:49] Carousel/slideshow weight example: 6 to 10 slides on the homepage, e.g. 5 images of about 2,000 px width, are loaded although nobody clicks through them; auto-advancing slides frustrate readers.
- [44:15-45:07] Theme update rule: if the store converts well and results are good, do not touch the theme; update when the store is 2+ years old (5 years = red flag) or was built by several freelancers; typical symptom: about 30% of the code is residue from uninstalled apps and about 15 JavaScript errors in the console.
- [58:23] Branding the checkout is cheap: "five minutes, worst case 15 minutes" of work to increase checkout conversion; stores often lose money by skipping it.

### Das 40-40-20 Prinzip (Course B) [L0408, 2021-22]

- [00:44] The 40-40-20 principle was developed by Ed Mayer, a direct response (direct mail) marketer active in about the 1940s to 1960s, who died in the mid-1970s, according to the teacher.
- [05:36] The exact ratio is disputed online, with alternatives such as 30-50-20 or 45-45-10. The teacher says the exact split does not matter. The takeaway is that about 80% of conversion chance does not depend on how the website looks.

### Das CURVE-Offer-System (Course B) [L0411, 2021-22]

- [12:15] The "free, easy returns" message was placed everywhere: in the ads, on the product page, on the homepage and on the about-us page, "you could not escape it". Because competitors only had it hidden, he was the only site showing it big and bold, and conversion changed massively.
- [10:22] Before launching free returns he expected "probably more returns" but judged that on balance it would produce more sales; he also committed to educate buyers about how things fit and how to measure.

### Der finale Call-to-Action: Kunden zur Aktion bewegen (Course B) [L0461, 2021-22]

- [08:11] Example CTA wording: "Hit the green buy now button below right now and we will ship your very own easy to install sauna to your front door today", tied back to the primary promise (convenience).
- [05:01] Real scarcity example: "we've only got 10 of these left ... otherwise it's going to be a six week wait"; real sale example: 30 to 40% off for the next week and not again for at least a couple of months. Fake sale, fake countdown timer, fake stock level ("five left" with 1,000 in the warehouse) and rolling "10% off until Sunday" are called dishonest.
- [09:20] For expensive products the CTA may be "give us a call today" with the phone number, because high-priced products often convert better by phone.
- [10:07] The example description comes to about 600 words (616 on screen), a healthy count to avoid thin content. The five extra bullets are split into short paragraphs and bridged with "The great stuff doesn't stop there. Here's 5 more awesome reasons".

### Die Customer Awareness Pyramid, Grundlage der Konversionsoptimierung (Course B) [L0481, 2021-22]

- [14:00 to 14:45] When many problem-aware visitors land on a product page that does not move them from problem aware to solution aware to product aware, there are two options: (1) segment your marketing so problem-aware traffic goes first to a more appropriate page, or (2) add content to the existing page so it walks them through those levels.
- [03:19 to 06:27] Worked example: a beginner who wants to surf; problems: no surfboard, in cooler water no wetsuit; problem turns into a want ("I need a surfboard"); solution aware = surfboard; product aware = a six or seven foot mini mal beginner board; ninja aware = knows the exact product and only picks the retailer.
