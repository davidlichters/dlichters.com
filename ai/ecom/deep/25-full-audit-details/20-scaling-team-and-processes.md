### 7.0 Introduction To Scaling Your Business (Dokument) (Course A) [L0190, undated]

- (doc, no timestamp, undated) Entry condition for Module 7 scaling: Modules 1-6 done and the store is live, getting traffic, generating sales and producing real income; the goal of Module 7 is simply to grow revenue (more order volume, better efficiency, maximize what already works).
- (doc, undated) Scaling does not mean starting over: you keep driving traffic, communicating with customers, processing orders and working with suppliers; the scaling strategies are layered on top of the existing workflow.
- (doc, undated) Named mindset shift: move from "Can this work?" to "How big do I want this to get?".

### 7.3 Scaling With Course A XA (Dokument) (Course A) [L0194, 2026+]

- Course A XA ("A" = Advanced) is an optional program for scaling beyond Course A; rollout begins late January 2026 and continues through February 2026; it will be announced on Discord. (doc, no timestamp)
- XA is for stores that fully implemented Course A and are live, getting traffic and generating consistent sales but hitting limits with core strategies. XA is NOT needed while you are still building or stabilising the store, still scaling profitably with supplier expansion, Google Shopping Ads and remarketing, or already getting the results you want. Course A alone is designed to be self-contained and enough to build a real business. (doc)
- XA content: advanced supplier acquisition (trade shows, relationship-based supplier strategies); advanced web design, UX and CRO tweaks; advanced branding and social media; advanced email (lifecycle and segmentation campaigns); SEO and content marketing; advanced Google Ads campaign types and tests for stores already doing $50k-$100k+/month that cannot scale Shopping Ads further at 10x ROAS; additional traffic channels: Meta (Facebook & Instagram) Ads, Microsoft Ads, influencer marketing, referral marketing; advanced automation and outsourcing (delegation systems). (doc)
- Consider XA only after maxing out Course A strategies, building a profitable stable store and hitting plateaus with current levers. (doc)

### Agentur-Einstellung: Finanzmodelle und Vertragsgestaltung (Course E) [L0217, 2025-26]

- [11:05] Worked example: agency USD 7,000/month managing USD 15,000/month spend at 2 ROAS = USD 30,000 revenue, while paying USD 22,000 (spend + fee) before COGS and OPEX, almost impossible to profit. Contrast: USD 5,000 to 10,000/month managing USD 100,000 spend at 2 ROAS = USD 200,000 revenue, much easier.
- [13:21] Do not judge in the first 15 days; it usually takes 30, 60 or 90 days to get into a good flow with an agency.
- [22:55] Correction/conflict: the teacher says creative is usually the best and cheapest role to bring in-house first: a USD 5,000/month in-house designer (USD 60,000 salary, fresh graduate) could produce far more than the 10 creatives an agency delivers for about USD 3,000/month. Fix.
- [21:58] Agency specialists are typically split across 3 to 5 client accounts; companies usually move off an agency one role at a time (first an in-house media buyer, then an in-house designer).
- [28:05] Real incidents among about 5 to 6 Course E member companies: a manufacturer took the money and ran, a Google account admin deleted everything, and someone deleted a Facebook account out of anger after the relationship ended. Hence always a contract, even for 3 or 6 months.
- [28:57] Minimum contract contents (no 40-page document needed): incentives, payment structure, timeline, performance expectations, cancellation terms (for example 30 days' notice). "Even marriages have contracts" (50% end in divorce).
- [32:34] A serious agency should not need more than 90 days to start scaling/testing; agree on the first 30 days of deliverables in writing.
- [33:28] Paying about USD 2,500/month for a Google Ads agency on an account spending about USD 5,000/month makes incrementality hard to reach.
- [41:39] Accounting of the fee: book the fixed base fee as a marketing expense in OPEX; a performance bonus based on contribution margin can be a variable cost above the contribution-margin line.
- [40:49] Percentage-of-spend example: agency on a percentage at USD 10,000/month spend that grows to USD 100,000+/month spend does almost the same work for far more pay; base fee reason: even at reduced spend they still upload e.g. 20 new creatives per month.
- [45:08] CRO agencies: one or two offer refunds if their individual split tests do not deliver incremental improvement; suggested structure = fixed fee for labour (landing pages, split tests) plus incentives on test performance. A tiered percentage-of-revenue deal (percentage falls as revenue rises, but applied to all revenue) looked "suspect" and agency-favoured; it might only be fair if they produced about 15 landing pages a month.
- [53:04] Creative spend minimums: some creative-agency heads run single ad set / single ad tests with 3 to 5 times AOV per ad. Teacher's view: state your testing method upfront; agreeing to a modest spend minimum per ad can be a small price for a long-term partnership.
- [50:30] If a creative agency fails for three months straight, that is a different conversation (re-evaluate).
- [04:49] Problem-definition examples: ad performance plateaued without in-house capability; nobody owns growth overall; ad-creative system broken (too few new creatives). Desired-outcome examples: short-term partner to recover a banned ad account; long-term scaling partner on a 1-year contract with plan to bring in-house in 18 months.
- [18:33] Time-value question: hours per week in the ad account (example 10 h = 40 h per month) times the hourly rate you would charge as a consultant (USD 250, 500 or 1,000). Extreme example: use the freed 10 hours to fly to China and cut COGS by 20%.
- [36:10] Example doctrine: Presenter E2's former agency ran everything on cost caps (cost controls) as its whole model.

### Aufbau und Skalierung von Remote-Teams (Course A) [L0263, 2022]

- [14:26] Agency contracts with half-year or one-year commitments are not recommended; agencies should only be tested once the business itself earns and pays for it.
- [18:19] Bookkeeper hired in the Philippines runs the books, but a certified CPA in the US handles tax; bookkeeper checks per order after the fact what shipping really cost (example: quote said $300, order turned out a $300 loss / the real rate differed), reconciled monthly.
- [20:15] Owner's plan: build the third store to sell it in about two years; selling a business with a big team and overhead is hard (Empire Flippers observation).
- [23:38] Many callers spending $5,000 to $10,000 online just want the call answered to verify a real business exists; no product knowledge needed for that.
- [48:54] Overdue-task metric can be manipulated by setting due dates into the future; that is why the "upcoming tasks next 8 weeks" view is kept hidden (only owner and operations manager).
- [55:06] Build the first raise into the starting rate; typical raise size 10%, 15% or 20%.
- [1:06:44] onlinejobs.ph candidate profiles show about 5 or 6 skill metrics in percentages (e.g. leadership) from tests; personality tests were not used.
- [1:12:47] Product video workflow: upload supplier video to your own YouTube channel and link it; to download a YouTube video, insert "pp" into the URL (youtubepp.com style trick learned from the listing team).
- [1:15:39] Give team members access gradually, only what is needed; one former team member downloaded the email list. NDAs are not standard, issued mainly when team members ask (credit/salary confirmation).
- [1:16:29] Audience note: store copying by competitors is more common than by team members; handle it via Shopify DMCA.
