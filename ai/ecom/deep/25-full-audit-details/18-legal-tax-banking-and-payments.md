### Betrugserkennung und strategisches Lieferanten-Sourcing (Course A) [L0294, 2025+]

- [04:43] Wire-transfer scam buyers usually want many items, e.g. "10 different things or 10 of the same thing"; a large multi-item order from an unverified inbound emailer is part of the pattern.
- [06:14] Variant of the overpayment scam on payment apps: the scammer insists on paying via Venmo, then claims "I just sent you money, send me back the difference", although no money was ever sent. Occurs even on mid-ticket orders of about $500.
- [10:02]-[10:47] Non-MAP products uploaded for credibility are listed at prices where they would be very profitable if they sell; no ad money goes to them, so any sale comes from free traffic with great margins, and no sale costs nothing. Ad money goes only to MAP suppliers as their approvals arrive.
- [10:02] Example of the credibility effect: after uploading the non-MAP brands, a preferred brand revisiting the site sees a store "selling for three different brands" with "maybe hundreds of products".
- [08:13] A 4-8 week approval timeline is unusual (teacher never received one); assume the supplier lacks manpower, accept it, note who you spoke to and when in the supplier tracking sheet, and set a phone reminder for 4 weeks.
- [15:10] Holiday promo timing example: run the Memorial Day promo Friday, Saturday, Sunday, ending Monday (Memorial Day) at midnight; these extra sales cost "basically $0 to acquire". Use lesson 6.7 "Creating Promotions" in the Blueprint; also plan something for June and the 4th of July.
- [00:55] Member result cited: Dan crossed $200K in revenue within a 30-day period (May 2025).
