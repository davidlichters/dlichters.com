### Branding und Private Labeling im Dropshipping: Vom traditionellen Store zur Marke (Course C) [L0324, 2025+]

- [03:08] If the product comes in several colours, you may need a different-colour logo per variant; the agent/supplier will tell you what they need, normally just the logo file, and they do the rest.
- [03:17] Private (custom) packaging is an extra cost on top of the logo; you can raise the product price to cover the packaging cost.
- [09:46] Consistency tolerance: within a 7-day week, 2 days may drop (example: $300 instead of $500) and that is still "fine"; only days at or near zero break consistency. Look for the rhythm over two weeks, seven days a week.
- [11:52] Below about 10 sales per week branding is not worth it (vs. 10 sales per day = go). For organic (TikTok) products be more patient before branding, because many of these products run only a few weeks and die out.
- [11:52] You do not have to build a brand at all: you can "take your money and go" if you do not believe in the product long term, even after it made money.
- [12:41] If you buy in bulk only to get faster shipping (no private label), you can start with about 10 units as long as you pay the fulfillment center's service fees.
- [13:31] The agent's job includes quality checks so the product matches the description on the store.
- [14:21] Pre-order example with numbers: jewelry store with 20 products, 8 sell very well, the other 12 sell occasionally; put those 12 on a pre-order model instead of meeting a ~200-unit minimum for each.
- [20:42] Amazon FBA risk: competitors pay people to file fake-product reports; enough reports take the listing and then the whole account down. Brand protection does not protect a private-labelled generic product; only a genuinely own product is safe. Amazon always sides with the customer, there is no dispute process.
- [29:01] Case: a friend's Amazon business (whole Walmart-style catalog, kids toys, kitchen appliances) was revoked with about $800,000 stuck on the account, not recoverable; contrast: Shopify Payments/PayPal can hold at most 180 days unless money laundering is suspected.
- [21:37] On Shopify you can dispute a chargeback; the investigation takes 60 to 90 days (speaker: "90 to 60 days, sometimes 90 days").
- [27:06] Buying in bulk and shipping from your own house is "a headache"; if you do it, batch all Monday-Friday orders (example: 28 orders) and hand them to DHL on Friday.
- [31:46] Crypto handling: of crypto payments received, they keep only BTC in crypto and convert the rest to fiat.
- [32:40] Keep PayPal: the speaker had no PayPal issues in the last 12 months by keeping shipping labels, tracking numbers and supplier names consistent (consistent, well-known suppliers).
- [36:18] Shop Pay vs Apple Pay (US): they deactivate Shop Pay for US customers because most of their customers pay with Apple Pay and Shop Pay adds an extra step before Apple Pay (bounce risk); they keep Shop Pay on for other countries where Apple Pay is less available. Shop Pay installments: Shop Pay acts as the lender, you get paid in full.
- [39:29] There are online services that act as a US representative for your company or set up a US company for non-US (UK/EU) founders for a fee; the speaker's Dubai company setup cost about 20,000 euros ("I think it's 20,000 euros to do in America"), possibly outdated vs. Dubai figures elsewhere ($10k-15k, L1512 2023).
- [41:11] Google Ads tutorial section was being added to the member area ("organic blueprint", "online advertising mastery" courses in the member section).

### Die echte Marke: Konkurrenz wird irrelevant (Course A) [L0487, 2018]

- [43:32] Niche-selection criteria for the speaker's homebrew brand: wanted a niche with recurring revenue (seasonal/holiday/sporting-event packages and a subscription box), room for educational content, and existing big stores in the niche being "boring, not educational, not fun".
- [43:32] Model site for content-led guidance: B&H Photo (New York camera store), "one of my favorite e-commerce sites".
- [41:52] Tool use for first YouTube content ideas: vidIQ (Chrome extension, shows trending videos and views per hour) and BuzzSumo for blogs; YouTube shows your video next to trending ones if watch time is longer.
- [10:20] Proof of brand-led content: in YouTube trending for the term CrossFit (top 10 videos of the past 24 hours), 8 of 10 came from people other than CrossFit itself.
- [44:55] Reason for a separate private-label brand name (not "Homebrew Lifestyle"): sell it inside the mix of known brands on the same site so it looks like a different company, then build it as a separate asset; also because own tested products were poor quality.
- [33:28] Gauge for brand success: other people sharing your content, using your hashtag, posting photos on your page, submitting videos. The Supreme-store-line analogy ([32:34]).

### Dr. Squatch: Fallstudie, Direct Response Marketing für ein Commodity-Produkt (Course C) [L0524, 2023]

- [32:44] Profit-estimate range: at a 3.5% conversion rate the estimate rises "up to 174" (K per month); if the average order were one 60 bundle instead of the yearly value it would be 54K; the teacher's overall range is "between 50 and 120" / "50 and 150" (K per month). Teaches: always give a range and rerun with different conversion and AOV.
- [05:40] Negative angle: besides stating what the soap contains, the ad message also names what is bad in the rival product class: shower gels with sodium laureth sulfate and parabens ("harsh chemicals"), pulling the buyer "back to the earth". Positioning = niche down (men) + natural ingredients + "negative" about competitors' ingredients.
- [07:18] Licensing examples: Spartan Scrubs (Spartan from Halo), Star Wars range ("Ruthless Rinse", "Dark Side Scrub"); the teacher also cites Udi's partnerships with Disney and Harry Potter as the same differentiation move.
- [37:17] Supplier tactic (CJ could not source an item): "keep CJ competitive" by telling them another supplier does it ("my supplier is doing it for this, I can't believe you can't") and push them; also ask the supplier agent publicly in Discord.
