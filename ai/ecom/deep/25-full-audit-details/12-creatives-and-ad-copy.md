## 12. Creatives and Ad Copy

Main chapter: https://dlichters.com/ai/ecom/deep/12-creatives-and-ad-copy.md

### Anzeigentexte schreiben für E-Commerce (Course B) [L0241, 2021-22]

- [07:21-08:32] Avoid any specific or general health, finance or "general life results" claims in ads; if you sell products that help people make money, improve health or make life more enjoyable, the teacher suggests promising nothing in the ad, because ad networks are very sensitive to claims that could be read as misleading, unsupported by fact, or not congruent with the landing page; ads making promises about money, health or wealth are "a really quick way" to get the ad account suspended. [2021-22]
- [05:23-05:29] The ad formula applies whatever is promoted: a blog content piece, a running sale, a newly launched product or a product from inventory; mention just enough benefits to get people interested. [2021-22]

### ChatGPT und KI-Tools für Copywriting und Produktfotos (Course C) [L0353, 2023]

- [14:41] Use case: ChatGPT wrote a customer-service email telling customers that copycats were entering the market, that the store is the original brand with no affiliated partnerships, asked to phrase it "in a delicate way so it doesn't look like we're showing off". (2023)
- [53:10-54:59] Engagement tactic reported from Luke Belmar (untested by the speaker): have a friend post a nice comment on the ad, then another friend posts a negative comment (e.g. "that puppy's ugly") to provoke an argument that drives engagement; higher engagement brings lower CPM and CPC. Speaker prefers simply making a good creative. (2023)

### Der Tod des traditionellen Creative Teams (Course E) [L0464, 2025-26]

- [04:08 to 05:56] Customer-research process of the human strategist, broken into tasks (a trainable/AI-able sequence): (1) identify existing avatars that already bought from the brand, (2) predict other problems/desires the product can fulfil and which people have them, (3) search online where those avatars share opinions or discuss related topics, (4) scrape those sources for raw data from real and potential customers (Facebook comments, Reddit comments, YouTube review videos), (5) summarize the findings and rank them by what is mentioned most often.
- [17:06] Own morals/red lines are legitimate (examples: never go after competitors, never mention a specific pain, never use certain kinds of AI, never make up a UGC creator), but "the fewer restrictions you have, likely the better your creative strategy is going to do, because you can test more things, and that is what platforms are looking for".

### Der Unique Mechanism: Logik im Produktkopiertext (Course B) [L0465, 2021-22]

- [03:47] Finding the mechanism as a reseller: if the manufacturer documentation does not show it, look at the competing products people consider, compare their features/attributes with your product's and take what yours has that others do not; a manufacturer usually knows it already.
- [04:34] It is fine to have one mechanism, or two, or three; the one thing must (at least in part) enable the product to deliver the primary promise made at the start of the copy, and it should support the opening story/thought command.
- [01:59] A product with no unique difference from competing products "is really a commodity-based product" and a "much harder prospect to market"; every product needs its own small USP beside the business USP.
- [14:48] Style: write conversationally as if to one person and use "you/your" a lot; short sentences and short paragraphs.

### Die drei Anzeigentypen: Esel, Zugpferde und Kriegspferde (Course E) [L0483, 2025-26]

- [05:59] Historical analogy for the workhorse/warhorse split: the speaker says there were "about 300 or 400 workhorses to every warhorse throughout history" and that workhorses do 80% of the work in the background ([06:50]).

### Die drei Hebel von Anzeigen und AI-Workflows (Course E) [L0485, 2025-26]

- [17:41] The speaker's "creative engine system" behind the custom assistant is described as proven "in $300 million in revenue over the years", with "thousands of lines of instructions" fed into the assistant ([17:23]).
- [19:39] Pre-built AI platforms named as examples: Arcads, Higgsfield and Motion (create ads from a brand website, or analyze ads / other people's ad libraries); the speaker says they are custom-assistant-like tools with built-in instructions, can partly be customized, and advises trying one for a month because "they all work pretty similar".
- [22:01] Coded-workflow tools: "We typically use N8N. You can also use Claude Code"; AI can build the workflow itself ("Claude in particular has been really great for that"), and the speaker still teaches manual building and sense-checking of workflows.
- [02:21] Rule for a workhorse: all three variables (idea, style, hook) must be easy to produce; if a scientific-diagram hook cannot be had easily, it is a warhorse.

### Die KI-Gelegenheit in der Creative-Strategie (Course E) [L0498, 2025-26]

- [02:13-03:17] AI is the biggest shift in creative strategy since social media; poorly used AI gives low-quality ads and worse results, especially after Meta's Andromeda update; platforms are wary of "AI slop", so only certain principles let some brands win.
- [00:48] Teacher credentials: Presenter E3, Course E creative strategy mentor, about a decade in creative strategy, own stores scaled to 7 and 8 figures, about $300 million revenue made mostly through creative strategy, his own agency, "tens of millions in revenue since AI".
- [03:29] Teaching approach: concepts and principles, not a single tool or affiliate software.

### Die vier Frameworks der AI-Kreativstrategie (Course E) [L0508, 2025-26]

- [09:16] Most brands only do conception and production and ignore or do poorly planning and management; the stages are defined as concept via research / plan what to produce / actually film, get creators or design assets / upload, check media buying, assess metrics and iterate.
- [07:44] Test idea, style and hook first because it lets Meta, "post-Andromeda", test very diverse ads.
- [00:00]-[00:52] Announced topics only (three types of ads, Warhammer 40k, two kinds of AI): no content in this lesson.

### Direct Response Copywriting mit Psychologie und Desire-Framework (Course C) [L0515, 2023]

- [32:10] Anchoring on price: the teacher says "gurus" do this in funnels: "a consultation call worth a grand", then "an ebook worth $200", "this course worth $20,000", "but for you $49.99".
- [32:55] Price anchors also work with subscription models: an anchored high price placed beside the middle option makes the middle option look like the best choice.
