### April 2022 Coaching Call: Nischenwahl, Google Shopping und Shop-Setup (Course A) [L0249, 2022]

- [05:19-06:13] Medical/home-care niches (hospital beds, wheelchairs, senior lifts): in a store the teacher built about a decade earlier, the hundreds of brands all went through one distributor, so he could not get approved directly and competed with the same catalog as many vendors; the store still did well and was sold. [2022]
- [11:29-12:19] Insurance cost example: the teacher's first general liability quote was about $1,000 per year; a year later a revenue-based audit billed "well over $10,000"; since then he uses a very small policy; members with big stores pay under $100/month. Get it through a big agency, not a small brokerage. [2022]
- [14:04-16:00] Alexa traffic data was being retired (end of May 2022); the Course A competitor research tool ([Course A research tools link]) still showed Alexa data and was to switch data sources within about two months. Possibly outdated. [2022]
- [30:32-32:27] Example: raising the daily budget to $150 to $200/day for three days gave traffic but no sales; advice: lower the daily budget back to about $20 to $30/day, collect data over a longer period, and find the CPC "sweet spot" instead of raising budget. [2022]
- [36:06 / 38:55] Course A done-for-you web design: $500-off promotion until Friday 11:59 p.m. Eastern (April 2022); a purchased package can be redeemed within one year; it includes setting up the product feed for Google Shopping but not running or managing the ads [1:01:08]. [2022]
- [57:53-58:40] Unbranded or made-to-order product types (e.g. home bars, garden sheds) are fine as long as you can still find at least 20 possible brands in total (mix of branded and unbranded); three makers for everything is not enough. [2022]
- [1:01:08-1:02:00] "People want to see/touch it" (e.g. surfboards): some will buy locally, but buyers who want a specific item not available locally or who know what they want turn to the internet; choose niches with so much variety that people must go online. [2022]
- [1:07:55-1:08:42] Standing discounts: promotions are cycled between dollar and percentage, but almost always percentage; 5% is not too small on high-ticket products; example "take 5% off all orders over $500" to limit it to a price range. [2022]
- [1:12:07] Dropship-friendly check via multi-category stores: a store that is online only, sells several niches, and has a subcategory (e.g. kayaks) with over 20 different brands counts, assuming other criteria are met. [2022]
- [1:22:10-1:23:00] A supplier website shown as "not secure" is fine to contact; security matters only where you enter personal/credit card data; supplier sites are often basic "business card" sites. [2022]
- [1:23:50] Competitor traffic rank: the lower the number (closer to 1), the more popular the site. [2022]
- [1:28:42] Affiliate program app the teacher uses: Refersion (Shopify App Store); no recommendation for a wholesale-program app. [2022]
- [1:37:04] Niching up by search volume: if individual home-furniture sub-niches each miss the 30,000 monthly search volume target, grouping related sub-niches in one store to reach it is "absolutely correct". [2022]
- [1:38:01] Strikethrough example: MSRP $1,200 is shown struck through and MAP $1,000 is the selling price; if the supplier has no MSRP, ask the supplier what strikethrough price they are comfortable with. [2022]
- [1:40:30] When niching up, drop any sub-niche that fails the price point test; do not sacrifice criteria to make other numbers work. [2022]
- [1:50:32] Fencing or flooring niches: same criteria, at least 20 suppliers and at least 100 products; products would most likely be bundled to reach the target price point. [2022]
- [1:51:26-1:52:18] Theme change and SEO: a new theme only hurts SEO over time if it is slower or has broken code; compare before/after in GTmetrix (grade and waterfall view showing what loads slowly). [2022]
- [1:53:15-1:54:10] Wholesale cost cannot be known before contacting suppliers; margins vary by supplier, e.g. supplier A MAP = 2x wholesale, supplier B MAP = 1.8x wholesale, supplier C wholesale $500 vs MAP $700 and wholesale $400 vs MAP $650. [2022]
- [2:00:38] Theme migration checklist: work in draft (unpublished) mode, click through every page (home, collections, contact, blogs, product pages) and go through checkout; e.g. Klarna messaging had to be readjusted because it did not show on the financing page. [2022]
- [2:01:26-2:02:16] Competing with a supplier that sells direct with big ad budgets: offer bonus gifts, and use a Google custom intent audience built on searches for that brand name with ads like "buy X and get Y free" (taught in the teacher's "retarget search terms" lesson on thebestthingilearned.com). [2022]
- [2:03:55-2:05:39] Taking Google Ads back from an agency: go back as far as possible in Google Ads, find the most profitable months for the store, check exactly how campaigns were structured and running then (via change history, since the agency may have altered old campaigns) and recreate that. [2022]
- [2:06:24-2:07:11] Customer seen in live chat failing to check out and not in abandoned carts: create a $1 product and buy it yourself, and repeat the checkout in the customer's browser/device (e.g. Safari mobile) from the chat data. [2022]
- [2:12:05-2:13:42] Free-shipping cost estimate: on freightcenter.com enter the supplier warehouse location (e.g. New York) and the product's shipping dimensions and weight, get quotes to random addresses in California, Florida and Texas, and use the average as the per-order shipping cost. [2022]
- [2:14:29-2:15:18] Brands with proven customer loyalty (proven by your own sales): add search text ads for the best keywords of that brand linking to its product pages, and/or break that supplier's products into their own alpha/beta pair to control and increase budget. [2022]
- [2:17:51-2:18:38] Pricing where MAP does not exist (e.g. UK): ask the supplier for a suggested price, check on Google whether people sell at it; without one, take at minimum 2x wholesale (e.g. $500 -> $1,000), but if competitors list at e.g. $1,200, list at $1,200 instead of undercutting. [2022]
- [2:20:14] Brands that send no CSV/inventory file and say "copy it from our website": not a red flag by default; often a smaller company without processes. [2022]
- [2:21:03] ClearSale fraud protection: the teacher does not use it; Shopify's built-in fraud detection is enough for most stores, though many members use ClearSale happily. [2022]
- [2:23:39-2:24:31] Changing the store domain: buy a new domain in the Shopify backend (about a $1 upcharge vs other registrars) and select it as primary; then update Google Merchant Center, Google Ads and add a new Google Analytics property. [2022]
- [2:28:45-2:30:30] Supplier dispute (price list "set of two" but only half the units shipped): the teacher would most likely eat the loss this time, get on top of all other orders, and possibly stop selling for that supplier; fighting for a credit or partial refund is a valid alternative for newer sellers. [2022]
- [2:33:45] Trademarking the business name: the teacher has done it only a couple of times across hundreds of businesses launched. [2022]
- [2:41:18] TaxJar does not issue a sales tax certificate; get it from the state's website. (The teacher said Florida has no sales tax; this is wrong, Florida has a state sales tax: possibly outdated/incorrect statement.) [2022]
- [2:44:35] 15% off MAP worked example: after the 2.9% transaction fee only 12.1% remains before free shipping and any ads; ask the supplier about volume pricing tiers. [2022]
- [07:42 / 54:35] Course A member meetup Tuesday April 12, 2022, 6 p.m. Eastern, Old Mecklenburg Brewery, Charlotte (about 25 RSVPs); next retreat planned in Lisbon, Portugal. [2022]
- [1:27:52] Planned mastermind format: at least two meetings a year with a small private group at the teacher's studio, everyone shares numbers on the big screen. Not launched as of April 2022. [2022]

### August 2020 Coaching-Call: Nische, Lieferanten, Store-Aufbau (Course A) [L0268, 2020]

- [07:01-07:57] Build the initial list of 20+ brands only from online-only stores; brands found on retailers with a showroom/physical location (a showroom counts as retail location) come later as follow-up, because approval rates are lower; some suppliers require a physical store (and, in the example, being based in Canada) for their dropship program.
- [13:40] The launch date / day of the month does not matter for the business; it only exists to build excitement with suppliers.
- [16:24] Sideways niches added to the store do not need their own full niche research; they are included in the primary niche's numbers.
- [20:19] Paused store: post behind-the-scenes updates and a countdown on social (30 days, 20 days, then 10 down to 1) and send biweekly update emails.
- [26:04] Lucky Orange is accurate, but cannot record screens where payment info is entered, so session drop-offs at the payment step are a recording limit, not lost data.
- [26:59] Mattresses are avoided as personal items, but baby mattresses are fine as an add-on in a crib store.
- [27:49] List the brand names you sell openly to capture brand-name search traffic; brands selling direct usually sell at MSRP while you may sell at MAP; one-up their product pages.
- [41:20] Selling products that are also on walmart.com is fine (Walmart online is largely a marketplace).
- [44:53] A niche at $100-175 average price is below the $200 recommendation; use it only as a supplemental niche without paid traffic, not as the primary niche.
- [47:27] Ubersuggest costs $29/month after the 7-day trial; you can revoke its Google account access after cancelling; speaker trusts Neil Patel's companies.
- [50:50] Example accepted as enough demand: 20 online competitors found, of which 25 (sic) meet the Alexa rating criteria.
- [55:19] Q4 is in general the busiest/most profitable period, especially November and early December (not true for every niche).
- [1:04:09] Asking a manufacturer to change its manufacturing process is private labeling, outside the blueprint.
- [1:05:02] Niche research time: a couple of hours up to 5 or 10 hours, "not something like a month".
- [1:14:33] Title to give suppliers on calls: speaker usually says "owner" (or president, whatever you are comfortable with).
- [1:23:30] Find at least 20 possible brands so that even 10 rejections on day one leave 10 companies to sell for.
- [1:33:48] Using supplier videos on your YouTube channel: ask the supplier for permission first.
- [1:38:11] No copywriter needed; conversion-relevant copy is the About Us page, shipping and return policy and price match policy (templates provided).
- [1:40:55] Wrong approach: 30% markup on $500 wholesale = $650 sale; with $100 shipping that leaves $50 before other expenses; aim for 30% net profit, not a 30% spread.
- [1:41:53] Supplier price lists may show wholesale $500 / MAP $1,000 / MSRP about $1,200, or only wholesale and MSRP plus a separate MAP agreement stating e.g. MAP = 2.2x wholesale.
- [1:48:59] Large/heavy items: avoid ones with MAP $500 and COGS $250, because shipping eats the spread; heavy items must be pricier so the wholesale-MAP spread covers free shipping and still leaves a 30% profit margin.
- [1:43:44] Uses a dedicated Google Shopping feed app rather than Shopify's basic Google Shopping app.
- [1:47:11] Brand loyalty test: bad when 3 to 5 brands own the market and buyers want those brands (watches, tablets, headphones); a known brand like La-Z-Boy recliners does not make the recliner niche brand-loyal.
- [1:47:11] Ask a few VIP social followers to critique the store before launch.
- [1:53:25] Subscription/recurring products were done about twice out of 100+ stores; treat it as a separate business.
- [2:01:25] Exclude products from promotions (e.g. a 5% abandoned-cart discount) only when the supplier says coupon codes cannot be used for that brand.
- [2:13:02] A supplier giving only 15% off is "not good"; during COVID 2020, slow supplier responses may be due to stock shortages, so pause that niche and move to the second idea.
- [37:51] PayPal funds can be withdrawn and deposited within 48 hours; everything was 100% financed by the business, no external funds.

### Bulletproof Produktforschungsmethode für Winning Products (Course C) [L0335, 2023]

- [17:58] Hit rate with this method: about 1 winner out of every 3 to 4 products tested (vs. typical 1 in 8 to 10).
- [04:26] A new competitor scaling for 1 to 2 weeks may have shown ads to only about 50,000 to 100,000 people, a small slice of a 10 to 100 million niche.
- [24:50] AfterLib: speaker took the higher plan at about $80/month; the two winners found with it in two weeks returned "almost 50x" the tool cost in profit. Free trial "7 or 14 days" (unsure).
- [29:19] Page-age threshold: a Facebook page created in the last 5 to 6 months (good sign "in the last five months") counts as a new competitor.
- [27:39]-[39:02] Worked examples: Cuddly Kitten ad with about 5k likes, 432 shares, 638 comments, page created 13 March 2023; another ad with 8k likes, 1k shares, 870 comments; AfterLib progression from 3 ads on May 25 to 88 ads launched on June 16; a page created 27 May 2023 already running 35 active ads within less than a month (strong signal); Lumidron page from 27 March 2021 went from about 30 to 57 active ads in one day.
- [43:31] Result detail: the two new products combined did about $3k to $4k on worst days and $6k to $7k on good days, projected $100k to $150k for the month; each averaged about $1.5k to $2k per day.
- [52:31] Supplier threshold: use Hyper Rescue until about 10+ orders per day, then switch to personal agents (speaker works with two agents, each with a minimum).
- [49:50] Spend-cap example for when an agency account makes sense: the ad account cannot spend above about $200 after all other methods were tried. Paid "Facebook insider" Discord services exist for limit problems (not necessary).
- [56:14] Earlier DropiSpy price about $20 to $25 (discount $15 to $20); a $20 to $40 tool investment can yield $500 to $1,000 profit per month.
- [1:02:33] Tool-quality check: within a 45-day window you should easily find 100+ potential products that competitors are scaling; if you cannot, the tool is weak, switch to the Facebook Ad Library.
- [1:08:46] Named rental site for tool logins: sharedtool.net; DropiSpy listed at about $2 (short term) or $10 monthly; covers about 90 to 95% of tools; AdSpy normally about $300; you can request missing tools (e.g. AfterLib) via their chat.
- [1:16:55] How sales spy tools estimate: they check how many visitors reach the store's thank-you page, typically for 1 to 2 hours, then extrapolate to 24 hours; no tool is 100% accurate because none has Shopify access.
- [1:17:49] Reference stores: Inspire Uplift, a general dropshipping store about 10+ years old, has had multiple $1M+ days; "Weird Girls" store showed about $1M in a day.
- [1:19:36] Scale benchmarks: $1M/day usually needs 30 to 40 days of pre-launch presence, an announced launch day and 10 to 15 celebrities promoting, and is not consistent; $100k/day seen on some stores; $3k to $10k/day is what the speaker does; acquaintances hit $100k to $300k/day; a store already at $300k to $500k/month with solid email setup can do $1M in a day on Black Friday/Cyber Monday.
- [1:21:21] Personal no-go niches: anything with glass, fashion (unless return/shipping policy is clear), health products with creams or chemicals.
- [1:23:08] Markup ladder: test at 2.5x to 3x, then raise to 4x and 5x as branding (custom images/videos) is added.
- [1:26:39] Afterpay: apply as a merchant at afterpay.com; approval takes 1 to 2 days, then add it in Shopify as an additional payment provider.
- [1:20:30] Speaker started dropshipping in 2021 and has done product research for about 2 years; he recommends sending product lists to mentors to check saturation.

### Coaching Call Juli 2019: Saisonale Niches, Google Ads Troubleshooting und Lieferantenstrategien (Course A) [L0357, 2019]

- [28:03-28:53] Inch-up worked case: two weeks running, under 300 impressions and about 10 clicks at a 10-cent bid. Raise to 15 cents that night, check impressions/clicks at the end of the next day, then raise to 20 cents, one step per day, until impressions, clicks and profitable sales appear. (2019)
- [1:06:29-1:07:07] Search impression share example: a report showing 15% means you appear for 15% of the searches you could appear for; use it as baseline for where budget is versus where it would need to be. Pull it after adding negatives. No fixed "good" impression count exists at 40 cents per click. (2019)
- [19:00] A UK-based seller can start in the UK; "we have lots of successful students in the UK"; speaker would just do business in the UK. (2019)
- [36:00-38:36] No-MAP suppliers: the most profitable ones were sold by "literally a handful" of other companies (example: about three, all with high markups); if 30 different companies sell a no-MAP supplier's products, making money is very hard. (2019)
- [34:01-34:47] A free PDF report with 237 niche ideas was shared as a niche brainstorming source. (2019)
- [1:21:26-1:22:20] If TSNN lists few trade shows for a country: Google "<niche> trade show Canada"; if nothing, go up a level (e.g. "home interior" or "interior design trade show Canada"). (2019)
- [1:18:10-1:19:00] Homepage slides: show what most people want, i.e. a running offer, bonus offer/promotion or bestsellers, because the homepage gets the most traffic; keep it simple. (2019)
- [53:59-54:49] Sales tax example: when the speaker's corporations were in New York, he had to collect and remit sales tax on orders shipping to New York. Speaker also believes any company running ads may have nexus everywhere because paid clicks can count (not legal advice). (2019)

### Coaching-Call: Nische, Lieferanten, Skalierung (Course A) [L0359, 2020]

- [17:46-20:49] Worked margin example (heavy items): gun safe A MAP $1,000, gun safe B MAP $400, same size. COGS at half of MAP: $500 / $200. Max ads at 10% of MAP: $100 / $40, leaving $400 / $160. Same freight cost $150 each: net $250 on the $1,000 safe versus $10 on the $400 safe. Lesson: the problem is too little spread between MAP and wholesale, not size. (2020)
- [1:16:34-1:17:24] Product upload quotes from overseas freelancers varied widely ("five for a hundred", "25 for 50", "40 for a hundred"); speaker says it cannot be priced generically: upload the first 10 products of each supplier yourself, time it (20, 40 or 60 minutes), and use that as the baseline for what the project should cost. Do not use Fiverr; use Upwork or onlinejobs.ph. (2020)
- [21:35-22:27] Case: a niche whose only Google Shopping competitors in Canada were Wayfair and Walmart, with no small niche stores; speaker: more good than bad, because products on walmart.com are mostly third-party sellers and these are not niche-specific stores. (2020)
