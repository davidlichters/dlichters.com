### Coaching Q&A: Produktforschung, Ad-Strategien und Fulfillment (Course C) [L0363, 2022+]

- [1:42:15-1:43:11] Pop-up timing rule: show the pop-up only after at least 15 seconds on the store, or after the store's average time on site plus about 3 seconds (an 8-second average means showing it at about 11 seconds; test between 8 and 11 seconds). (2022+)
- [1:06:50] If creative metrics fall under 2% (spoken as "CPM of under two percent", meaning CTR), work on more creatives, because better creatives improve conversion rate across the whole funnel. (2022+)
- [1:01:26] Facebook CPMs were said to be back to normal, similar to 2019 or 2020 levels after the tracking-loss period. TikTok CPMs will rise as competition grows. Possibly outdated. (2022+)
- [1:00:31] About 50 to 60 mentorship members were running Facebook ads at the time with few ban problems, because they followed the ban-prevention practices. The two reasons given for Facebook's poor reputation were bans and tracking loss (about a year without a fix). (2022+)
- [1:08:40] Expect a winner after testing about 7 to 8 products, with the fundamentals in place. the Course C teacher's most-viewed one-week dropshipping challenge tested 3 to 5 products and was not profitable in that week. (2022+)
- [1:07:47-1:11:21, 1:49:37] Recommended YouTube learning sources: the Course C teacher (dropshipping challenges, strategy, case studies), Raphael (spoken "Slintron"; tax, legal tax-free setups, shipping times), Biaheza (biggest dropshipping channel, about 1.4 million subscribers, one- and two-week challenges), Course E (branding, plus a detailed SMS-marketing video with an SMS agency expert), Dara Denney (creatives and ads), and a "Disruptor" channel for Facebook ads. Watch case studies and challenges, and avoid trick channels such as the "TikTok glitch method". (2022+)
- [1:45:50] SMS example: "Valentine's Day VIP sale, extra 50% off", with event and offer in the first one or two lines. About 90% of people see phone notifications, which is why SMS beats email open rates. (2022+)
- [1:49:37] Despite recession fears, 2022 did better than 2021 in e-commerce, with a smaller but positive increase. (2022+)
- [1:30:56-1:31:53] Slow scaling example: at about 500 USD revenue per day, about 100 USD is profit. Reinvest it and add about 500 USD of outside cash per month. (2022+)
- [1:28:00] The teacher lost about 8,000 USD at the start (Facebook account banned, tests including Google Ads, no mentorship). Strict rules, fundamentals and a system beat guesswork. (2022+)
- [1:34:55] For TikTok, unlike Facebook, the launch time matters. Some advertisers name 6 a.m. to 12 as a good time. Facebook ads are launched at 12 a.m. (midnight). (2022+)
- [57:48, 1:39:29] Channels the teacher used: Facebook, TikTok, Google and Pinterest (good recent results), and Snapchat once without scaling. (2022+)

### Demand & Evergreen Tests zur Nischenprüfung (Course A) [L0451, 2020-26]

- [06:53] The demand tool returns the range the search volume falls into, not an exact count. Two different niches (chandelier and standup desks) therefore show the same 165,000. Teacher: as long as the number is well above 10,000 the niche passes, and equal numbers across niches are normal.
- [09:20] Niches that fail a test (trampolines: seasonal, 301,000 searches, $334.24; motorcycle helmets: seasonal, 201,000, $246.53; tripods: 74,000 searches but average price $68.02) are marked red and moved to the bottom of the worksheet, not deleted. Reason given: keep a record of every niche already researched for future stores 2, 3, 4, 5. Winners stay on top.
- [03:56] The research tool has a country dropdown, default US. It must be set to the country where you plan to sell, not where you live.

### Dropshipping-Geschäftsaufbau: Q&A mit Course A Januar 2020 (Course A) [L0551, 2020]

- [06:50] Niche verification in the members area (Blueprint > "get your niche verified"): guided form, team replies within 24 hours, Monday through Friday, with green light or pivot suggestions; do module 1 first (1:06:10).
- [08:55-09:55] After holidays: keep building the supplier list and demo store, start calling suppliers the following Monday when everyone is back (Jan 2020 timing, example only).
- [10:40-12:27] Product-name format on the store is not a fixed formula: for each brand, check the top one or two resellers on Google and copy their title order (product name only / brand + product / with SKU).
- [16:51] Ignore CPC from Ubersuggest at niche level: ads are bid at product level (e.g. "Sony 65-inch"), so CPC is much lower.
- [22:55] Inch-up example: budget $20/day -> $25 for a few days -> $30 -> $40, check each time that 25% more spend gives about 25% more clicks and sales. First days: "don't go too low on CPC", about $1; ads start within 2 to 3 days of campaign setup; $20/day with $1 CPC example (28:13).
- [32:52-35:28] Starting budget: at most $33/day (or $20/day); Facebook remarketing budget about 10% of Google (example: $200/week Google -> $20/week remarketing; transcript says "20%" then "10%" and ends on 10%); bid for reach not conversions: with 1,000 visitors a conversion objective would show to only about 50.
- [40:34] Social minimum: only a Facebook page with about one post a week from the start (needed for remarketing and as proof of a real company); not Instagram/Pinterest/YouTube/TikTok/LinkedIn from day one. Social is not needed to start selling.
- [43:58] Google Shopping CTR guide: close to 5%, minimum 1% (case: 2,000 impressions, 2 clicks = 0.1%); below 1% add negative keywords; if keywords are good but CTR low, search them in incognito and improve your listing vs competitors' listings; wait a couple of weeks (about 14 Jan) in the slow post-holiday period before judging.
- [44:48] Skip supplier directories and paid sourcing services; the Blueprint (module 2 research, module 4 approval) gives better suppliers.
- [49:52] Pre-load demo products with royalty-free stock photos (e.g. 5 surfboard pictures) until the first supplier approves; not trying to deceive, just so a supplier sees real product pages.
- [50:48] If a brand sells direct to the public below your price: do not upload if known beforehand; if found later, keep it on the site for organic traffic but exclude it from Google Ads.
- [51:55] Teacher's typical start: about $33/day, about $1,000 for the first month, profitable during test.
- [55:32-57:11] Heavy product math: a home gym at $2,500 with wholesale about $1,250, shipping about $400, order processing fee, ads about $100 gives about $700 net; at $500 sale price with free shipping it fails; heavy items need high price points. Wholesale should be about 50% of sale price (also at 1:17:25); a 30% supplier promo leaves too little (organic only).
- [1:01:35] Supplier website traffic/Alexa rank does not matter for suppliers (they want retailers to get the traffic); it matters only for future competitors.
- [1:02:30] Klarna not showing in store: follow Klarna's Shopify docs, then Shopify live chat, then email Course A.
- [1:04:27] Inbox Funnels = advanced Klaviyo automated email flows program by the teacher; not needed at the start.
- [1:10:28] Rule for a second store: do not build store 2 until store 1 does at least $50,000/month in sales with Google Shopping at least 20% margin (about $10,000 net/month).
- [1:07:50-1:15:44] Case of 15% margin and sub-1% conversion: check Google Shopping ad conversion specifically; at 15% gross margin prefer organic/SEO (module 6) and run paid ads only on suppliers with better margins; pre-buying inventory only when selling so many that stock is needed, never just for a discount.
- [1:12:14] Better Legal: members area > Bonuses; no service fee, only state filing fee.
- [1:12:14] B2B niche is "no better or worse" than B2C.
- [1:13:02] Wayfair is fine as competitor; extract suppliers from Wayfair, not Amazon.
- [30:28] Menu order: best-selling category first, descending best to worst; few sub-collections, same order; minimalist menus (48:14).
- [1:16:32] Speed check with GTmetrix (free); uninstalled Shopify apps can leave code behind, ask the app developer which code to delete.
- [1:19:12] Idea sourcing: Kickstarter for trending high-price products, then find other brands making them.
- [26:26] Net 30 terms: never offer; use third-party financing (Accept Financing lesson, v8) so you are paid on day one.
