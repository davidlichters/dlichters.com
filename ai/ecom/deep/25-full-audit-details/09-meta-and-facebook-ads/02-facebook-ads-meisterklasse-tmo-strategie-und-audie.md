## 09. Meta and Facebook Ads (page 2 of 2)

### Facebook Ads Meisterklasse: TMO-Strategie und Audience Targeting (Course A) [L0643, 2015]

- [42:24] The presenter and a partner invested $36,000 to improve their sales process ("a big chunk of money") because it would take them to the next level. Used as the argument for investing in learning or hiring.
- [25:07] Facebook holds roughly 1 million data points on every person (family, restaurants visited, location and so on). Used as the reason to use broad data targeting such as income, household size, political party and last car test-driven.
- [29:19 to 30:09] Instagram ads had just launched on 30 September (2015) and were tied directly into the Facebook ads platform with the same targeting. The presenter had already spent heavily testing them. For visual products he recommends skipping Facebook and going straight to Instagram.
- [38:10] Cautionary example: a dropshipper had six-figure revenue but only about $15,000 profit because he did not know his numbers. The lesson is to know profit per customer, lifetime value and maximum acquisition cost.
- [33:09] Source of the add-to-cart optimization tip: a Shopify store owner who spends $1,000 to $3,000 a day on marketing said optimizing the pixel for add to carts gives the best actual conversion rate on the back end, and that most people optimize for conversions instead. The presenter says to change running ads to add to cart (valid only with a follow-up sequence for cart abandoners).

### Facebook-Ads optimieren: Creatives analysieren, Zielgruppen verstehen, budgets skalieren (Course C) [L0644, undated]

- [45:50] Benchmark from the mentor's own account: the store page converts at about 3 to 4%, yet the CTR is seldom above 1.5%. His conclusion is to keep improving the middle section of the ad, because more qualified clicks are worth more than a higher CTR. He would rather have 1.5% of well-targeted traffic than 3% of time-wasters.

### Facebook-Ads Skalierung: Von Testing über CBO bis 100k Umsatz (Course C) [L0645, 2023]

- [1:03:38 to 1:04:36] During the iOS update (the pixel was unreliable for about 1 to 2 months) the mentor downloaded all sales data from his store as a CSV, uploaded it to Facebook and the actual ROAS showed in Ads Manager 1 to 2 hours later. Facebook learns from the uploaded purchase data.
- [1:04:36 to 1:06:19] Pixel data safety: some advertisers duplicate the pixel to a second pixel from the start (tools such as RedTrack) to keep the data. The mentor has not seen a lost pixel in 2 years of ads. If it happens, Events Manager and the account still hold the data and support can restore it. In Events Manager you can compare the purchases the Shopify Facebook channel app sent against what the pixel tracked.
- [41:44 and 1:09:04] Engagement numbers: about 2k+ comments on the ad within 2 to 3 weeks. Each ad has about 2.8k likes and about 1,000 comments, while the page itself had only about 100 likes and 100 to 150 followers in 1 to 1.5 months. The CPM fell from $15 to $5 to $8 and later to about $5 to $6.
- [39:59 to 41:44] Comment seeding format: from the brand page post a comment like "What our customers are saying", then a quoted testimonial, then five stars, then a name with country (e.g. "Christina, USA"), plus an image if possible. Add 2 to 3 such comments. Zero comments make people distrust the ad and stop asking questions; 50 to 300 comments invite questions.
- [38:22] Benchmark: TikTok CPM is normally about $3 to $5 to $7, which is why people move to TikTok. The mentor's Facebook campaign reached about $5 to $6, while a brand-new creative started at about $20 to $30.
- [1:09:04 to 1:11:44] Facebook page: about 10 to 15% of customers check the page, but most dropshipping buys are impulse purchases. He scaled to $100k in 30 to 40 days with only 3 to 4 posts. A healthy page should show about 10% engagement, so a 100K-follower page should have 6,000 to 10,000 likes per post. He bought about 5,000 followers plus 10 to 15 posts on a store that did well (cost under $50, possibly about $10) purely as a trust factor.
- [10:42 to 12:41] Day-by-day testing data (ad sets at $15 per day): day 1 ROAS 6.97 on only 2 purchases (AOV about $95 to $96), the next day 3.39, another day 6.05. About $45 spent per winning interest over 3 days, and 3.28 over 3 to 4 days of data. Rule: single-day ROAS on 1 to 2 purchases is only a test signal, and you need 3 to 4 days of data and 3+ purchases before launching CBO.
- [28:52 to 30:47 and 32:41] CBO budget ladder: raise by $100 per day while profitable (100, 200, 300, 400, up to 500), at most one increase per day, only after about 12 to 14 hours show 3+ ROAS with 3 sales. Three CBOs at about $500 each give about $1,200 to $1,500 per day. Apply the increase to one CBO first, then to the others.

### Facebook Ads Testing, Optimierung und Skalierung (Course C) [L0648, undated]

- Example from 13 October: three CBOs with 4 to 5 ads at $200 each; later CBOs ran at $300 each. Three CBOs hit three times as many audience pockets as one.
- [1:20:03 to 1:21:49] Triple duplication was developed by advertisers spending $50 to $100 million a year on ads. The cost of testing 6 CBOs would be about 6 × $20 = $120 before any kill, and the mentor has never tried more than 3.
- [1:06:18] The audience testing sandbox of a product that has run 7 to 8 months still holds 100+ active ad sets (assets), and 15 to 20% of the budget still goes to testing audiences or creatives.

### Facebook Advertising Playbook: Von 50 auf 5 Stunden pro Woche (Course A) [L0649, 2019]

- [48:40] Static hot-traffic remarketing is NOT segmented by audience type or buying reason. The speaker says they tried different messages for the reason people might not buy, but do not segment. They used to segment (surfers got a "book on paddle boarding" ad, paddle boarders got a "new book" ad, same product). It worked, "but we don't do it anymore and it still works better"; "Facebook's just so much more advanced that for now it's not necessary".
- [47:47] Creative formats: upload a standard landscape video (not selfie/tall) plus image versions and let Meta pick the placement. The Meta option "customize this for each platform" exists but they have not used it, to keep things simple.
- [46:43] Meta's own ad-creation tool has built-in templates with the correct dimensions and matching text backgrounds, and images can be uploaded there.
- [44:48] Goodwill/promotion exclusion remark: speaker says "we do exclude" a recent-customer audience ("it's only seven days") in some ad sets. Wording is garbled in the Whisper text, so the exact rule is unclear; it conflicts with the "do not exclude customers" rule for promotions at [29:12] (possibly a different campaign).

### Facebook-Anzeigen-Analyse und Angebote-Optimierung (Course C) [L0650, undated]

- [06:24] Scaling example: the speaker's own brand currently runs with two interests at $200 (per day) and "it's still going very very well" after raising the budget 10 → 20 → 50 → 100 on the same ad set.
- [43:14] The Course C speaker offers a 12-step video on how to safeguard the three Facebook assets (personal profile, page, Business Manager); he says it does not guarantee 100 % protection against bans.
- [41:31] Speaker's own result after doing business verification: "almost one and a half year now" without any ad bans (his personal profile had been banned before). Only a different lesson with a 13-step verification and about 1.5 years is in the notes.

### Mentor C3 Q&A: ROAS-Schwellwerte, Skalierung und Black Friday (Course C) [L0692, 2023]

- [04:10] Student offer example: single product 50 dollars; buy two get one free; final bundle buy three get one free plus the other product free; teacher: test lots of offer structures ("covered" only partially: the buy-3-get-1-free+other-product-free structure is not recorded).
- [07:06] Student sells to Sweden, supplier says 4,000 units sold that month; teacher: check the Ad Library for Sweden to see whether anyone runs the same ad and copy the competitor's copy but localised; Klarna-type local payment option is required (Klarna covered, the Ad-Library-per-country check and "copy the copy but make it Swedish" are not).
- [16:26] Scaling rule detail: with break-even ROAS 1.5, teacher waits for six to seven sales ("let's make it seven"), minimum ROAS 1.7, and at that point only a small test such as a $50 CBO; "higher is better, but nothing lower than 1.7".
- [47:10] Scaling set-up: one CBO with four ad sets and three creatives per ad set, duplicated three times, all started at 12 a.m. ("four ad sets with three creatives each inside every CBO" and "start at 12 a.m." are not recorded with that structure).
- [15:50] Student test: six audiences at about $26 each (= break-even cost per purchase); two audiences with good CTR were well above break-even ROAS, the other three/four were bad; teacher: if one creative takes the majority of the spend and is bad, switch off that creative and let the others spend the break-even cost per purchase; if spend is then even and still bad, switch off the ad set.
- [41:50] Student case: first three days conversion rate over 3 percent, then doubled budget and add-to-carts dropped to zero; teacher advice: stop the ads and relaunch after one or two days.
- [38:22] Price example: bathroom cleaner sold at 80 EUR with 25 percent offer, active ads only six; speaker estimates ad spend 500 EUR per ad; without the branded page it would sell for at most about 40 EUR.
- [33:03] Teacher: a boring problem-solver product like this can make "maybe 20, 30, 40, 50,000 per month"; photos can be shot on a rented Airbnb wall for one day with a phone and edited in Canva.
