### Analyse von Facebook Ads Testingkampagnen mit Regeln und Skalierung (Course C) [L0229, undated]

- [08:06] When replacing a failed interest with a suggested one, the replacement interest shown ("living room") had an audience size of 115 million, judged "the size is good".
- [10:03] Second replacement example: suggested interest "flooring" with 36 million audience size, described as "a decent size" (36M accepted as fine).
- [10:09] After creating replacement ad sets, "remember to check date of launch of these ad set": the old ad set is turned off only after the new one is live, and Rule 3's 24 hours are counted from each ad set's own launch date.
- [11:37]-[12:31] Rule 4 worked example: break-even ROAS 1.3; after an additional $60 (2x the $30 break-even CPP) per ad set, carpet ROAS 1.2 -> turn off; floor ROAS 1.6 and mop ROAS 2.0 -> winning ad sets.
- [13:30] Bundle offer examples for a no-sales campaign include "buy one get 20% off, buy two get 40% off" (tiered percentage bundle) besides BOGO and buy 2 get 50% off.
- [15:26] Ad spend analysis example: of $30 ad set spend, $27 went to video one (about 90%) with no sale and bad CTR -> turn video one off and let videos two and three spend another break-even CPP.
- [17:36] If CTR across ad sets is fine (examples given: 1%, 1.2%, 1.5%) but no ad set makes sales, review the whole funnel, mainly the website, starting with a $1 test purchase.
- [18:22] Inspect the clear display of shipping times on the store, and that no surprise shipping charges appear at checkout; never charge any amount not mentioned on the product page or in the ads.
- [19:15] Free-shipping split test method: include the shipping cost in the product price (free shipping) and compare against charging shipping separately.
- [20:28] Free ebook is listed as an offer idea alongside the $1-$2 free gift, bundles (single/duo/trio via bundle offer apps), BOGO and buy 2 get 1 free.

### Bezahlter Traffic: Die richtige Plattform wählen (Course C) [L0296, 2021+]

- [03:42] Paid traffic scales fast: from $500/day to $1,000/day to $10,000/day or more "within a span of a week or two", which organic routes cannot do.
- [04:31] Further paid options named beyond the six main ones: Snapchat ads and native ads.
- [12:27] TikTok ads boom started around 2020-2021 with very cheap clicks and traffic, which drove many advertisers from Facebook to TikTok; now TikTok CPMs are "almost similar" to Meta. Possibly conflicts with the same lesson's "TikTok CPM $10-15, cheaper than Meta" (13:16), which the teacher also says is getting more expensive year by year.
- [12:27]/[13:36] How far a TikTok product scales depends on product, niche and price point; products for youth / younger demographics who are active on TikTok work best and scale "pretty much similar to Facebook".
- [19:45] Exception to "Google is weak for top of funnel": if people already search the use case (example: a knee-pain product and people searching "knee pain"), Google can show ads to people who have never seen the product.

### BFCM Q&A: Facebook Scaling, Supplier Management und Holiday Strategie (Course C) [L0298, 2023]

- [01:32] Only evaluate a Meta performance drop if it is consistent for more than 3 days; short dips are normal.
- [05:18] For high-budget scaling, set up the "four main rules" for high-budget campaigns (video by Nick Theriot, recommended by the mentor) so you do not overspend without sales; only after these are in place raise budgets above 20%.
- [19:41] Daily Fulfill (agent) is good but normally works only with stores doing at least 15-20 orders per day.
- [19:41]-[20:33] CJ is good for the initial phase; later its service is "not at par" depending on product; Zendrop, USA Drop and similar companies are "almost similar, not that great". The mentor's own private supplier (1.5 years) gives better prices, shipping and service.
- [42:42] For a cheap product ($15 single unit, a toy), aim for an AOV of at least $30 (i.e. push buyers to 2 units), alongside the minimum of $20 margin or 3x markup.
- [44:29] Ways to add perceived value to a cheap product's offer: add a free item, better imagery, or an ebook.
- [45:20] Bundle vs Monster Cart test: run the bundle option for about 7 days, then Monster Cart for 3-4 or 7 days, and keep whichever gives the higher AOV (they cannot run together).
- [49:59] Localized store detail: use yourdomain.de, or .co if .com is the main domain. With Shopify Payments you can use Shopify's native translation or apps (e.g. "Transi" as spoken) in the same store; without Shopify Payments the checkout stays English, so a separate store is better.
- [39:46] Benchmark: a creative CTR of 2% or more is good; with normal CPM and such CTR, CPC lands under $1.
- [29:58] Case: a winter-shoe product moved to a Sweden-only store got CPC down to $0.30 and CPM to $15-20 but no sale after 3 days of spend, so it was killed.
- [30:55]/[32:03] Case: toy test copying a competitor with 30 ads in the ad library, 5 ad sets at $7 each, targeting top-5 countries; 3 sales from one ad set on day 1, but CPC $2-4 because CPM was $55 with CTR 2.05%.
- [38:34] After seeding own comments/reviews, expect better CPMs after about one week.
- [55:39] TikTok cutoff rule example amount: with spend of about $30 (set at 2x break-even cost per purchase) and no sale, cut.
- [1:03:10] Add an offer banner (e.g. "Early Black Friday Sale", "Halloween sale ongoing") to creatives that have run for more than two weeks, to lift CTR.
- [1:04:31] Testing budget split: 20% of budget to testing in total; 5-10% to audience testing if you still test audiences; if the audience is proven and only CTR is dropping, 15-20% to creatives only.
- [1:05:22] Creative test math: 2 concepts x 3 hook variations each, about $30 per video/ad set, run for 2 days; hook variations differ only in the first 3-5 seconds.
- [1:08:27] VIP early access: land VIPs on a separate special Black Friday page or collection page with the offers 24 hours before the public sale; no truly special access needed.
- [03:14] Student case: a jewelry store built a unique "free gift bundle" as its marketing angle and reached CAD 3.8k/day sales with 100+ orders on about CAD 1,000/day spend.

### Black Friday: Lead-Gen, Emails und aggressive Budget-Skalierung (Course C) [L0308, 2023]

- [28:11] During the scaling night ROAS never fell below 2.0 against a break-even ROAS of 1.5, so the margin check was rarely needed.
- [24:36] Doubling continued until about 4 grand was spent on that ad, and the rule of thumb: if the 4-hour ROAS is only one or two decimal points above break-even, do not double; lower budget back and take the higher ROAS.
- [10:52, 23:12] Video creatives: besides top and bottom banners, a diagonal "sash" banner across the 4:5 video was also used.
- [16:13] Abandoned-cart first email copy idea: "Looks like you forgot something, we've saved your item in the checkout for you", with a timer saying the item is held for 24 hours. Possibly outdated: newer deep sources (L1208, 2025-26) say not to word reminders as "forgot something".
- [35:02] Shopify pays out a few days after sales, but sales from a weekend arrive on Wednesday; plan leverage for that lag.
- [36:50] Black Friday weekend economics: 76k revenue was roughly 10k profit per day.
- [29:59] Customer service sizing: going from about 200 profit/day to 800-2,000 profit/day on Black Friday is manageable; the 76k weekend was not, with 5 people.
- [39:51] Best ad creator cost about 3,500 EUROS per month.
- [43:02] Billo pricing: about $77 per 15-second video, 4 x 15 s for about $300 per 60 seconds; teacher paid 120 "bucks" for one 30-second portrait video.
- [38:53] A UK limited company owner not living in the UK: credit check runs against the company but personal guarantees may be checked against the person; teacher did not know.

### Creatives und Hooks: Struktur, Testing und Optimierung für Facebook-Ads (Course C) [L0398, 2023+]

- [03:03] Numeric illustration of Meta's first test: the ad is shown to about 1,000 people; e.g. 500 engage and 500 do not; a weak creative might get only 300 engaged, a really good one 500 to 700. "Engaged" here means e.g. viewing 25 to 50% of the video; Meta then optimises toward the profile of those engagers. (2023)
- [05:14] Teacher's own consistently performing scaling campaigns ran for more than two to three months with little optimisation and then died because of creative fatigue (not the audience). Fatigue can also come after two or three weeks or within the first month. (2023)
- [25:55] Big brands spending heavily (example store doing about 1 to 5 million USD per month) test about 10 to 15 or 20 creatives at a time; look for the one creative that shows up in several ads. (2023)
- [26:48] Pattern example: a gadget / camera-accessories brand (name recalled as "Clam core") used the same first-3-second scene in every ad: turning a light on and flashing it on someone's face, with different persons and angles while the later clips changed. Replicate such repeated hook scenes. Second example brand checked in the Ad Library: Kitty Spout. (2023)
- [28:34] If the hook makes people stop scrolling and watch, "50% of your work is done"; the hook (first five seconds) is the most important element to copy from big-brand patterns. (2023)
- [48:19] CPM of about 35 or more: first suspect a competitive audience; some niches such as beauty are really expensive; low engagement on the ad also raises CPM. (2023)
- [48:19] For the first product test, include at least one creative that is exactly or nearly the same as an existing winning competitor creative (with small changes: logo, metadata, details). Teacher's example: a lightly changed copy of a competitor video was his winner, earned about 6k to 10k likes and ran for almost two to three months. Then make two or three variations of it. (2023)
- [50:05] Benchmarks with exact wording: outbound CTR above 2% is good, 1.5 to 2% acceptable in some cases, 2% is the usual Facebook average; CPC below 1.5 is good "in 2023". If CTR is 1.8 to 2% but CPC/CPM are very high, Meta is penalising a forced creative: make new creatives, try once more, otherwise switch product; also try UGC/TikTok-style creative, which shows "crazy CTR". (2023, possibly outdated)
- [54:30] Concrete hook phrases from the teacher's hook list (for TikTok and Facebook): "The perfect [X] doesn't exist", "Top TikTok shopping finds", "If you haven't tried [X], watch this" (suspense/curiosity), "Getting my daily [eggs/vitamins] hits different with [product]", "TikTok made me buy it", "3 reasons why I love [product]" (named one of their highest-converting ad creatives), plus "Amazon finds" and "Wish I knew earlier" [28:34]. (2023)
- [57:27] Hook-rate example: of three tested hooks, one with a 60% hook rate is performing really well; reuse that hook and change only the later clips. (2023)
- [57:27] Click path to create the metric: Ads Manager columns > Create custom metric > name it "Hook rate" > format Percentage > formula 3-second video plays divided by impressions > Create metric. Hold rate is built the same way with ThruPlays divided by impressions. (2023)
- [1:01:16] UGC sourcing: the team (based in India, unable to film products locally) hires creators from the UK, US and Canada via platforms such as Billo and Insense; the raw-clip method saved them more than 1,000 USD per month (teacher also said "in a week"). (2023)
- [1:02:10] Creator tool named: Modash; alternative is direct Instagram search for niche creators with about 3k to 50k followers; also hire someone on Billo, then message them on Instagram directly for ongoing content. (2023)
- [1:08:46] On Billo, finished output is usually a 30-second video; use the chat option to ask for the raw clips instead. Some UGC creators (trend under #ugc / #ugccreator on Twitter) now charge more for raw clips; argue that raw clips mean less editing work for them. (2023)
- [1:11:26] Covering a competitor logo: teacher uses e.g. a red background bar with white text on top to hide it. (2023)

### Facebook Ad Bans verhindern: 13-Punkte-Strategie (Course C) [L0637, 2023+]

- [16:13] Business verification was the turning point: after verifying, the teacher says he was not banned for 1 to 1.5 years; before that four of his profiles were banned (ad account restrictions, profile bans, BM bans).
- [20:26] Facebook Advertising Policy Blueprint is a ~15 to 20 minute beginner course ("policy, data privacy and ads"); the certificate is pasted into an appeal asking for manual human review.
- [35:22] Teacher's own target for page feedback score: above 4 (scale 0 to 5); 3 to 4.5 is the normal dropshipping range.
- [42:40] Appeals: about 8 of 10 times a bot replies "nothing we can do, final decision"; an "ultimate hack" article with hundreds of canned replies is used to force a human reply; the teacher says appeal multiple times. Not found (possibly deceptive tactic, outside platform terms).
- [44:30] Permanent ban fallback taught: new laptop/system and different IP, brand-new profile (from family/friends or a service like ACC farm), then follow all 13 steps. 07-ban-recovery says the opposite (do not evade a permanent ban): possibly outdated / conflicting.
- [48:08] Agency accounts: teacher says he has not seen much difference in quality versus normal accounts, but they "never get banned" and, if banned, return within 1 to 2 hours or next day.
- [57:19] Reason for the 250-ads-per-page/account limit: Meta's "Performance 5" article (account structure should be 2 to 5 campaigns, simple), so Meta limits active/in-review ads; fix = turn off non-performers and reuse the post ID. (Limit and post ID covered, the reason is not.)
- [54:35] Facebook scale path with consistent rules: 100 a day to 500 a day, then 2,000 / 5,000 / 10,000 a day once audience and creatives are good. Not found with these steps.
- [55:29] TikTok creative cadence by goal: 100k-300k/month needs a new creative every week; 10k-30k/month a new one every 2 to 3 weeks.
- [53:44] On TikTok non-beauty products (life hack, home decor) rarely work above about $60. Teacher reached ~$1k/day on TikTok.
- [1:05:56] Teacher's history: started October 2020, first ~$10k via a gifting-niche product, found a winner in June 2022 after testing a few products, about $300k total ad spend; most money from the electronic gadget niche, pet niche also worked well, health and wellness "great". Electronic gadgets covered.
- [1:08:46] Funnel after a test win: after one week of good results move from the general store to a one-product store, then add upsells/related products and email marketing, then branding.
- [1:23:55] Metadata trick: change video speed from 1.00 to 1.02 or 1.05 (e.g. in an online speed-changer or editor); visually identical but metadata differs, so TikTok may not recognise the video.
- [1:19:14] UGC sourcing prices: Twitter/X #ugc / #ugccreator creators give a video for about $10 to 20 in exchange for a review/testimonial; Billo raised prices to $59 / $69 and quality was uneven. (Hashtags and $10/creative LinkedIn covered, the $10-20 Twitter figure and the Billo $59/$69 are not.)
- [1:25:41] Customer-success VA hiring: started at $200-250, raised to $300 per month; six-round interview; a Google Form is sent to ~90% of applicants and removes about 50% of them (asks salary expectations, experience, eagerness to learn); then a Zoom interview, hired at $250; onboarding with Loom recordings, one week of shadow testing, full duties from week two.
- [1:33:01] Bulk-ordering inventory (Amazon / own stock) cuts supplier prices by about 50 to 60%, so margin rises if the sales projection of the last 2 to 3 months is consistent. (Forecast covered, 50-60% price cut not.)
- [1:12:17-1:14:01] Google Ads: ROAS 4x to 7x covered; additional claims not found: some stores got "almost 30x" from bidding on competitors' brand terms when the competitor did not run Google Ads; of 100 visitors 2-3 convert and ~10-15 more are interested non-buyers that Google retargeting/search can capture.
- [24:08-25:03] Warm-up note: the teacher does not warm up most of his own accounts; starting directly with $5 to $10 conversion campaigns is acceptable as long as you do not go aggressive (no 10-15 ad sets on day one).
