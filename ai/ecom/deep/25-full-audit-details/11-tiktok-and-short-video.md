## 11. TikTok and Short Video

Main chapter: https://dlichters.com/ai/ecom/deep/11-tiktok-and-short-video.md

### Black Friday und Weihnachtsverkäufe mit TikTok Ads (Course C) [L0309, 2024+]

- [15:18] Bulk-stock case: they ordered about 2,000 units to avoid over-leverage, demand reached about 6,000; their supplier found other manufacturers last minute and saved about 4,000 orders.
- [37:19] Organic account kick-start: around day 2 or 3 put $20 to $40 of paid boost on the account and let it spend within 5 to 10 minutes, once only; views pick up a few days later. Teacher says it only speeds up the answer, not the result.
- [42:32] Moving-on rule (Mentor C3): if fewer than 1,000 views within 24 to 36 hours, start moving on; if still not 1,000 to 1,500 views after 3 to 5 days of testing, drop the product.
- [46:52] The best-performing account was about one week old with zero uploads before its first video.
- [12:42] Store-credit tactic: if the customer says the store lacks what they want, say you own another brand, ask what they want, and source it from AliExpress or another supplier to fulfil privately.
- [08:23] Forecast in that call (2022): a credit crisis likely somewhere in 2023 or 2024; spending drop expected mid to late 2023.
- [29:18] Agency ad accounts are needed for Facebook but not for TikTok.
- [05:40] If serious about Q4, have a store ready within the next week (early November); after Black Friday and Cyber Monday keep promoting for about two weeks.
- [02:47] Brands already advertise Black Friday and Christmas months before the event; check the ad library for their copies and videos.

### Die Organic Testing Machine: Systematisch Produkte auf TikTok testen (Course C) [L0501, 2022]

- [08:26] A new TikTok post can show up with a delay of about 1.5 to 2 hours (zero views in that time is normal), views normally start within 24 h.
- [18:08] A scenario video took 3 weeks to go viral; staying longer than 1-2 weeks is only worth it if you KNOW it is a winning product that solves a problem; a pure "wow factor" product with no views after 1-2 weeks should be killed (a few thousand to tens of thousands of views in the first 1-2 weeks expected).
- [30:06] Kill/new-account rule: if videos did 5,000 / 4,000 views in week 1 but by end of week 2 only a few hundred, TikTok has stopped pushing; start a new account for the product or kill it.
- [21:30-22:22] Revenue per million views: US/UK/major countries about $1,000 to 3,000 per million (up to $3,000-4,000 with a strong angle); elsewhere (third-world) a few hundred, around $100 to $200 per million on some products; about 10% conversion of the US level; lowest end can fall under $100. A video with 3.6M views, 50% US, gave only about 5 sales per day.
- [24:03-24:55] Separate view-videos from sales-videos: a 20M-view video at about $500/M gave about $5,000 in sales, a 10M-view video at $3,000/M gave about $30,000; cycle view-getters and converters. A student with 7M views got only 300-400 orders.
- [25:47] Monkey toy account: a little over 10M views gave only $1,800 (product not content); they posted daily anyway. A sponge-product student had millions of views with poor conversion due to extreme view-angles.
- [27:30] Targets: average at least $1,000 per million; 500 or even 200 per million can work. 10M views per month at $5,000 sales and 50% profit = $2,500, fine. 100M views in a month at $500 on average = $50,000 per month.
- [28:25] Over 10,000 followers gives credibility; with 50 or 100+ videos old videos carry the account for weeks and months; about once per week an old video goes viral again.
- [29:17] Best niches of the teachers: health and fitness, clothing, beauty (beauty is competitive and hard).
- [31:51] Re-launch idea: a good product that got no views in Aug/Sep can be brought back in Q4 (November).
- [30:58-31:51] The same strategy works for software services and personal brands (a student promoted software with 3 videos a day); an account with 34,000 followers can be sold even if the product fails; few accounts ever reach 1,000 followers.
- [33:50-34:44] Coins can be switched on/off, left over coins carried to another video; goals: website visits or video views; store must be ready before posting. Coins do not need big spend: $10 lasts about 2 days.
- [36:06] Custom coins audience: remove minors; 18-24, 25-34, 35-44; sometimes only 18-24 and 25-34 because they buy more; gender only for gender-specific products; otherwise leave interests broad.
- [37:55] Coins on an already viral video: $10 test possible, shows like a TikTok ad with button (Shop now) to the website; teachers only use coins mainly on new accounts and where angles already work.
- [40:45] Coins example: $10 paid about 2,000 forced views, then about 8,000 more organic because TikTok already knows the audience.
- [23:14] Build the store BEFORE going viral: people who saw videos before a link existed rarely come back; no link on profile loses returning customers.
- [47:30-48:23] Dummy-order check: of 7 products tested only 1 refunded; others claimed "already shipped" within 30 seconds. Preference: products with around 34 orders per 24 h (monkey product 2 orders in 24 h was dropped). A low last-24h count may only mean the videos died down.
- [52:42] Video mix examples: of 3/day one conversation/follower video, one copy of a competitor's viral video, one own test; or 2 similar to what works + 1 different angle; with 4/day 2 similar + 1 proven riskier + 1 new riskier.
