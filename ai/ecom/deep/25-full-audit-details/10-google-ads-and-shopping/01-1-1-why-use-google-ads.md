### 1.1 Why Use Google Ads (Dokument) (Course A) [L0013, undated]

- (doc) Google Ads reaches people who are "raising their hand", i.e. actively needing a solution; Google Ads is a large share of Google's business and of the screen real estate after a Google search.
- (doc) Reasons listed to use Google Ads: control is in your hands, you know how much you make from each ad, Google support is available. Tools linked in the video: SearchScientists.com, AdBadger.com, RealTraffic.io, WordStream.

### Advanced Google Ads Playbook: Vier Kampagnentypen für Kalt-, Warm- und Bestandskunden (Course A) [L0210, 2022]

- [02:03] Performance Max (2022): hit or miss; some stores put a lot of money into it without payoff ("learning forever"), so Course A did not teach it yet. Smart campaigns were being deprecated that month and converted to Performance Max. App and local campaigns not used (no apps, no physical stores). Possibly outdated.
- [01:15] Google Shopping (with Shopping remarketing) still gives the best ROAS and is where everyone should start; the 8 extra campaigns come after Module 6 is complete.
- [25:30] Exclusion window for monthly-promotion campaigns depends on repurchase: if buyers will not buy again within six months, exclude 6-month (180-day) customers; if the product may be bought again after about a month, exclude only 30-day customers.
- [24:43] One Display campaign with static images is pushed out automatically as a standard display ad, a Google Discover ad and a Gmail ad.
- [23:54] Monthly-promotion budgets are set from audience size and historical CPMs (Course A uses a Google Sheet for this).
- [28:19] Run the brand-name Search campaign "sooner rather than later, even with a very small budget": anyone with traction will see competitors appear on a search for their store name (some enter it manually, some via Google associating the name with the niche). Example: a "Lamps Plus" search shows Lamps Plus first, then competitor ads (Shades of Light, others) before the organic result.
- [29:52] Brand Search setup details: no goals guidance, results goal "website visits" with the homepage URL, conversion signal Purchases, bid strategy Impression share > Absolute top of results page > 100%, max CPC limit start USD 5 (can be more; will rarely spend that much), set country where you do business.
- [37:36] Extensions: no call extensions; sitelink extensions only on the store-name campaign (contact us, privacy policy, return policy, best sellers); no sitelinks on top-product campaigns, to keep people going straight to the product.
- [36:48] Generic cold Search (non-brand, non-product keywords) is much harder to make profitable and is mostly not done; the next step up is a brand-of-product campaign sent to that brand's collection page, which is rarer than per-product campaigns.
- [35:51] Product Search ad example: pinned headline = product name ("Ali crystal chandelier"), next headline "in stock and ready to ship, free shipping and returns"; description includes the price (example USD 1,680) so it reads like a Shopping ad without the picture.
- [40:32] YouTube video placements: keep only "YouTube search results" and "YouTube videos" on; skippable in-stream counts down 5 seconds; ads also show on Google homepage/feed.
- [45:13] Dynamic video remarketing ad: skippable in-stream, URL = store, call-to-action "Shop now"; the ad shows the four most recent products the person viewed, each linking to its product page. Video creator template needs 3 images, a logo, text, and lets you set brand hex colours.
- [46:43] If a video remarketing campaign gets expensive and stops converting, use the campaign-setting frequency cap (maximum impressions/views per user per day, e.g. once or twice per day).
- [48:25] Frequency-cap rationale example: a 180-day audience of 100,000 people, few of them on YouTube, would otherwise be shown the same ad over and over during a 4-day promotion; cap at 2 per day.
- [47:30] Video promotion schedule example: Halloween October 28 to 31; ads start at midnight on day one and stop at midnight October 31.
- [50:03] The evergreen /promotions page stays live between promotions with a standing offer (example: 5% off all orders with a coupon code).
- [53:21] Goodwill video audience: "Purchased 7 days"; if too small, expand to "Purchased 30 days" at most, never longer (do not thank people 180 days later). Use a real phone-filmed video (backyard/desk), not the generator; one video runs forever. Course A does not measure its effectiveness and says it is not a difference-maker, only a unique touch (anecdotal reviews/feedback).
- [55:51] Start order: finish Module 6, create the GA audiences, then launch campaign 1 (dynamic display remarketing) first because it has the most reach; add the others one at a time with a small budget; once set up they need little maintenance, monitor every couple of weeks.
- [1:00:47] Discovery promo ad example: headline "Course A merch Halloween sale", description "Get Dropship Secrets for free when you spend over $50, from today through October 31st".
- [1:03:51] Website-visitor and customer audiences are "invisible lists": people join them without opting in, so they need no email capture or request.
- [1:05:55] If YouTube ads did not bring sales: first use the dynamic version with the product feed linked; start small; expect results similar to your Facebook remarketing and use that as the baseline for spend vs sales.
- [1:08:31] At scale Course A still uses the Alpha/Beta Shopping structure, just with more Alpha/Beta campaigns segmented by specific products, categories and brands.
- [18:45] Teacher's answer on exclusion size: match the excluded customer window to the campaign (30-day remarketing excludes 30-day customers).

### AI-generierte Anzeigentexte und Produkttitel für Google Ads (Course E) [L0220, 2025-26]

- [02:14] RSA copy prompt inputs: the website/landing page URL, a campaign or ad group theme (demo: "low calorie sauces"), and target keywords; use a validated keyword list from the keyword research step. The prompt first outputs a landing page summary (brand, product, category, customer intent, conversion action, core USPs, trust signals, promotions, key benefits) and extracted USPs, which you can correct/steer in the same chat. (2026)
- [03:46 screen] Demo AI output structure: 15 RSA headlines = 10 keyword/relevance headlines + 5 urgency/promo/CTR headlines (e.g. "Over 11,000 Top Reviews", "Free US Shipping Over $40", "Save 15% On Your First Order"), each with its character count; descriptions: 4 total, 3 keyword/relevance. The prompt also suggests headlines to pin. (2026)
- [04:00] With this AI copy process the teacher's search campaigns have reached Quality Scores of 9 out of 10 "or even 10 out of 10"; still apply your own judgement because AI output can be "ridiculous really bad" at times. (2026)
- [02:48] It does not matter whether you use Gemini, Claude or ChatGPT for the prompt; output is similar. (2026)
- [04:27-05:19] On Search the account enters each auction with a single keyword/asset combo and appears once; on Shopping the same advertiser can appear multiple times in the same row. (2026)
- [06:54 screen] Compliance example: a brand marketed as "diabetic friendly" should phrase cautiously ("supports low-sugar diets", "diabetes-friendly condiments" used carefully) and avoid "healthy" as an absolute claim. (2026)
- [07:26] After generating title variants, ask the AI to isolate the attribute keywords into one list, append a product-type word (e.g. "keto friendly sauce", "sugar-free sauce") and check them in Keyword Planner for search volume before choosing the title. (2026)

### Alpha-Beta Strategie für Google Shopping Kampagnen (Course A) [L0225, 2015+]

- [01:50] Bidding aggressively on high-converting (alpha) terms lets you show up more often and in more spots: "maybe two or three or four" of the Shopping placements for one search instead of just one.
- [17:34-18:29] Choosing the first target ROAS: take the break-even ROAS and "push it up a few notches" (example 1.67 break-even -> 3.3 target); yours may differ by business type (dropship, wholesale, high-margin electronics).
- [16:34] Campaign name can also carry the country (e.g. "USA") when running multiple countries, or state-specific offers, in addition to type, category and target ROAS.

### Anruf-Tracking in Google Ads einrichten (Course A) [L0234, 2020-26]

- [00:15]-[01:08] How dynamic call tracking works now: each visitor gets a Google forwarding number, and a returning visitor sees the same number on every visit (older systems needed hundreds of bought numbers, one per concurrent visitor). The number carries the traffic source into Google Ads (examples: social, organic, YouTube, business card, Gmail ads).
- [01:28] Use Google's free call tracking when paid traffic makes up the big majority of clicks to the site; use a paid multi-channel tracker only if all traffic sources must be tracked.
