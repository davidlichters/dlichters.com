### Amazon FBA: Private Label Business Model und Markenaufbau (Course A) [L0227, 2014]

- [51:57] Typical supplier pricing tiers to request in the tiered pricing sheet: 1 to 500, 500, 1,000, 2,500, 5,000, 10,000 units. (2014)
- [50:55] Teacher's first supplement order was 500 bottles and it sold out; stock-outs were the biggest early problem and cost rankings. (2014)
- [52:52] Ask in the first email whether the supplier allows private label; teacher's own lead time is about 6 weeks; 2 weeks would be great; a 2-month lead time is "not ideal" but scares off competitors. (2014)
- [15:05-15:53] As brand owner with exclusive distribution you can stop unauthorized resellers on Amazon by sending a cease and desist letter citing trademark infringement; non-brand products (e.g. DSLR cameras) face 10 to 20 sellers competing on price. (2014)
- [15:53] Example: a seller went from about $25,000 revenue per month to $140,000 within three months when his product took off in the rankings. (2014)
- [34:21] Teacher ranked a product on Amazon page 1 with only 3 reviews because of high sales in a short period: sales velocity, conversion rate and FBA drive ranking more than review count. Their worst daily conversion rate was about 13%, still "well above 10%". (2014)
- [35:56] Amazon PPC: one product's ads were profitable, the other's broke even; both kept running to hold sales momentum and ranking and because supplement buyers reorder. (2014)
- [44:00-46:57] FBA fees: rule of thumb "around 20%" depending on category is not accurate; use the FBA calculator. Worked example: lid sells $16.99, Amazon leaves $11.05; with $3.00 product cost, profit $8.05 per sale (~50%); if product cost were $8.00, profit ~$3 = 17% margin: do not take the product. People with very cheap China sourcing reach 70 to 80% margins; with only 10 to 15% margin serious marketing is hard. (2014)
- [39:46] Coupon-site launch discount must be big: "like 50% off"; 10% off "no one cares". As brand owner you can even sell for $1 to get people using it. (2014)
- [41:34] Cheap differentiators: add a free bonus item (e.g. a bonus tail light with a mountain-bike light) or nice packaging (box instead of flimsy plastic bag) to raise perceived value. (2014)
- [03:28 / 57:10] Online commerce was only 10 to 15% of total commerce at the time ("now is the time to get in"). (2014, possibly outdated)
- [21:06] BSR sales volumes differ by category: same BSR means much higher volume in Cell Phones, lower in Automotive; a top-level BSR around 40,000 means roughly one sale every three to four days. (2014)
- [29:04] Teacher removed HTML colours from the description and conversion did not change. (2014)

### August 2021 Coaching Call mit Course A: Mitgliederfragen beantworten (Course A) [L0270, 2021]

- [12:35-13:26] Juggling a full-time job with building the store: Course A worked from a local library (laptop, a couple of hours on weekday evenings plus weekends) as a dedicated work space; a coffee shop or a park with phone tethering works too. Getting out of the house matters most in the beginning, before money comes in.
- [23:03-24:46] No-sales audit, step one: review all historical Google Ads data (which ads and keywords get impressions, which get clicks, where the budget goes). Example: if about 70% of the budget goes to keywords that are too broad to have buying intent, add them as negatives or lower the budget of the campaign where they appear. Step two: open the product pages that get the clicks next to the top two or three competitors' pages and work out why someone would buy from them instead, then optimize those pages.
- [24:46-25:37] Google Search Console says "sitemap could not be fetched" for three weeks: fetching a Shopify sitemap should take no more than about 10 to 15 minutes; contact Shopify support on live chat so they can check the site and fix the code.
- [25:37] A business registered in Germany can apply to suppliers in an English-speaking country by presenting its German business information; a local entity in that country is not required in general (varies by country).
- [29:12-30:07] Accessory suppliers that do not dropship and require a $500 minimum order: not recommended, because you would need a fulfillment center or ship from home, i.e. more work and extra cost for less profit per sale. Instead ask your existing suppliers whether they offer lower-priced, niche-related products outside the main niche.
- [32:49-33:43] Do not outsource calling suppliers (Course A will never offer it): the relationship between store and supplier is too important to have anyone in between. If you prefer email because of English skills, write a draft supplier email and ask the community (Facebook group or forum) for feedback on grammar.
- [35:27] A cheap low-ticket dropship store bought on Fiverr: Course A would not use it (maybe sell it back); start the blueprint store instead.
- [37:24-39:12] Selling a store: Empire Flippers had done over $305 million in website sales; for a high five-figure or six-figure store use a broker (lists of ready buyers). If the goal is a big payday, set a sale date about six months ahead and push as hard as possible while keeping the profit margin as high as possible, to get top dollar.
- [46:23] On state business-registration forms, describe the business as "online retailer", "retail" or "e-commerce"; Course A has never written "drop shipping" on any business form.
- [1:24:39] Taxes when living in an RV or abroad: from Course A's understanding (not legal advice) the state that counts is the one you are a resident of, roughly where you primarily spend your time and hold your driver's license; people from high-tax states such as California change residency to another state before moving abroad.
- [1:44:14, 2:11:50] (possibly outdated) The new Course A Shopify theme was in beta on about 20 live stores in August 2021; it was rebuilt from scratch without SCSS (which Shopify was deprecating), loads faster and builds in features that otherwise need third-party apps (an availability-date feature was requested).
- [1:46:21-1:47:12] Timed promotions (special price for one week, then back): Course A does not change prices; he uses coupon codes, because most suppliers enforce MAP and accept coupon codes but not lowered list prices, and codes are "more exciting" for shoppers. Automatic timed price changes would need an app or one of the top-tier Shopify plans.
- [1:48:58] US suppliers do not care whether your business is Canadian or US-based; they care whether you will bring them sales and take care of their customers.
- [1:56:01-1:56:55] A "no chargeback" policy on the store would most likely not help win disputes for "item not received"; Course A assumes card processors do not allow it.
- [2:09:06-2:10:06] Paying companies that protect you from chargebacks: Course A does not use them; personal decision (see his video on preventing chargebacks).
- [1:58:34] When a supplier wants the dealer application sent by email after the call: on the call ask "who should I direct the email to?", then open the email with "Hi, I just spoke to you on the phone".
- [2:04:46] Startup cost list (2021) also included using the $100 Google Ads ad voucher in addition to domain (~$12), first Shopify month ($29) and LLC (~$100).
- [2:07:28] One store is more than enough to make money: Course A members run single stores doing millions in yearly sales and hundreds of thousands of dollars in profit.
- [2:13:40-2:14:31] Course A's start with a $1,000 credit limit: he put all orders on the card and paid it off every single morning from the previous day's merchant-account deposit, and kept calling the card company, which kept raising the limit.
- [2:27:51-2:28:42] Supplier outreach follow-up: if 10 of 30 emailed suppliers responded, follow up with the other 20 (emails may be unseen or in spam); keep following up after a few days of silence before looking at sub-categories.
- [2:32:16] Ignore Google Ads' recommendations to increase your budget; almost always they just mean spending more money. Course A ignores them almost every day.
- [2:25:07-2:26:58] A niche that boomed during COVID and is now slowing but still meets all criteria: keep going and adjust, do not quit ("accept the randomness": results can dip for weeks without any change on your side).

### AutoDS-Gründer: Produktrecherche und Skalierung im Dropshipping (Course C) [L0280, undated]

- [19:51] Budget split rule: 10 different creatives at $1 per day each ($10/day total) "you're not going to get very far" because $1 per ad creative is too little; split your budget according to how many creatives you have. With a low budget, start with 3 creatives (at $5 to $10 each).
- [20:46] Around day 3, if an ad shows some potential but "you're just not there yet", give it another day. Then turn off the ads that do not work and launch new creatives that "follow the same path" as the one that is almost working.
- [21:25] Do not put the whole test budget in on day one: if the product fails you have wasted all your money and cannot test new products.
- [14:32] Cheap products are easier to sell than medium to high ticket; medium to high ticket is "much, much more profitable", but first get sales and traffic, so start cheaper.
- [16:08] (host) Make the product hard to price-compare: rename the product and use your own photos so shoppers cannot Google the name or reverse-image-search it ("one of one").
- [28:52] Branded products while dropshipping are hard: manufacturers usually will not brand a product unless you buy a minimum order quantity, "which means you're not dropshipping anymore".
- [29:42] Quality checks only become possible once you buy the product yourself from a manufacturer (e.g. Alibaba), and only after the product was tested via dropshipping; buying inventory before testing is a financial risk that can end the business if it fails.
- [41:54] Case detail (host): Mentor C1, met in London two years earlier, has converted his $2.6M-revenue Shopify store into an outdoor brand and will probably sell it within the next two years.
- [35:23] Store build time is secondary ("you can build your Shopify store in two hours... in 10 hours"); reinvest profits back into the business especially in the beginning; keep more than one product on the list even if you are sure one will work.
- [33:10] ClickFunnels vs Shopify: the guest uses ClickFunnels less than PPC plus Shopify and says both can work, Shopify being easier and cheaper; the host says ClickFunnels is outdated, better funnel builders exist (an unnamed French one), and big brands such as Gymshark and Kylie Jenner's cosmetics brand do not use it.
- [25:24] Image-only ads: the guest does not run them himself; he has seen some sellers succeed and others not; video is better.
- [08:43]/[09:03] eBay wording in the transcript: "You can only list, like, 10 products" and "you can only list 10 products per day".
- [16:53] Success definition example: some count $100 at month end as success (up from zero), others call anything below $2,000 not success; the guest measures growth.
- [37:41] Host background: started his first online business at 15, now 24/25 (about eight years); worst-case framing for a 45-year-old beginner: five years to $300k to $500k profit.
- [04:08] Student research tools named: Afterlib, Ads Manager / ad library, Peeksta; product on the market 3 to 4 years with 3 to 4 big brands, not marketed in non-English European markets.
