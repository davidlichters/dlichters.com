- [07:48] Returns experience: CJ would not take returns; setting up a returns system would have cost more than absorbing a 5% return rate, so initially refund without return; later stricter for repeat multi-item returners (clothing).

- [24:05, 39:34] Product page speed: reuse a template in the library with PageFly (slots about $20/month), copy the page, swap images and copy; testing without a product page only via engagement ad on up to four products.
- [27:00-28:50] Meta ad tax on UK/EU registered ad accounts; a student claims a CPA said changing the ad account country to United States or Pakistan avoids it; teacher not an accountant (possibly risky/outdated).
- [30:38-33:15] Supplier list named: CJ, Hypersku, USA Drop, Zendrop; USA Drop sent the wrong variant causing chargebacks; CJ sent about 300 units with another brand's label "nearly crippled" the store; instruct suppliers to contact you before any substitution; manufacturer found on Alibaba in Pakistan needs MOQ prepayment.
- [16:46-17:37] Too-new product: agent orders sample, films close-ups (stitching), then order one; run a small test, shut off after 2 to 3 sales if quality poor.
- [1:00:02-1:02:44] Saturation workaround: take a common product to a niche segment (snowboard market), angle e.g. scalp tension / hair-loss for head massagers; period belt switched to "gift for fellas" and reached eight figures.
- [1:04:19-1:07:47] High ticket ($200 to $400) needs advertorial/landing page educating, YouTube video then retarget viewers of 75%, five-part email series, start with lower-ticket item then upsell; motorcycle gear $120 with $70 to $80 cost lost to returns.
- [58:12] Battery products cannot be shipped to the US.

### Dropshipping-Stores bewerten und gewinnbringend verkaufen (Course A) [L0553, 2018]

- [26:43-27:35] Single exclusive supplier relationship (supplier partner sells only through you): a buyer wants a contract that the supplier keeps selling; best: set up a second supplier and place a few orders before the sale, then hand over as backup; otherwise the buyer may discount or not buy.
- [30:28] Seller is not liable if a supplier leaves after sale (buyer beware, due diligence by buyer); Empire Flippers vets listings before approval.
- [32:13-33:06] Separate a wholesale/private-label supplier business from the retail store; do not sell as one business (conflict of interest).
- [34:51-35:43] Buyer personas: Lifestyle Larry ($200,000 to spend, store at $8,000 to $9,000/month profit), DIY Dave (about $40,000 site to tweak), Strategic Sally (existing e-commerce wanting scale), newbie buyer (needs more support).
- [40:52] Buyer-side representation: Ace Chapman (acechapman.com, Web Equity Show podcast); Empire Flippers represents sellers only.
- [16:23] Email can add about 10 to 20% revenue boost, raising value.
- [21:10] Of 26 sold, nine took longer than two months; the rest (about 60-something percent) under 60 days.
- [05:41] Seller who thinks the store peaked at $10,000/month often sells to a buyer who plans to double it in two or three years via untapped channels (Instagram/Facebook).
- [03:02] Typical seller motives with numbers: first store at about $1,500/month sold to fund second store at $5,000 to $6,000/month; wedding needs $30,000 to $40,000; adoption $20,000 to $30,000 (a $40,000 affiliate site sold).
- [01:26] Empire Flippers 2018 stats: 26 stores sold in 12 months, 50+ team, on track for $35M sales in 2018, $53M total since Q1 2015.
- [28:29] Typical post-sale support baseline: email support for about a month plus two calls of at least an hour.

### Mentor C3 Gritsenko: Von €0 zu €215k in 11 Monaten - Dropshipping Case Study (Course C) [L0690, 2022+]

- [03:08] Mentee case numbers: started the mentorship in December 2022; the first money-making product started on 18 May; about 11 months in he is at roughly 215,000 (EUR) revenue, of which 120,000 came in the last four months.

- [29:42] Teacher's advice on going full-time: he knows dropshippers who earn enough to go full-time but keep a (low-hour) job, because income is guaranteed; one month can be very good and the next very bad; a small low-hour job covers the consistent bills; do not go full-time until you can hit the monthly minimums comfortably. Mentee: for the first 8 months of his product he feared every week it would die, so he plans a second/third product or store to feel safer.
