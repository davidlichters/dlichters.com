## 03. Suppliers and Fulfillment (page 1 of 2)

### 4.0 Introduction To Getting Suppliers (Dokument) (Course A) [L0108, undated]

- Module 4 key principle stated as a formula: "Approvals = Trust"; trust is named the single most important factor in supplier approval (brands are trusting you with their reputation).
- Order of operations: do NOT call or email suppliers immediately; first build credibility (registered business entity, professional business email, separate business phone, demo store) with the explicit goal of removing all red flags before first contact, which the lesson says dramatically increases approval rates.

- [00:28]/[01:15] Upload cadence: as soon as a supplier says yes and sends product information, upload its products right away, then go back for the next approval; repeat the approve-then-upload cycle five times (goal of the module: first five supplier approvals) before moving on [13:23-14:10].

### April 2024 Coaching Call: Lieferanten, Shop-Setup und Ads (Course A) [L0253, 2024]

- [01:58] Canada-based seller using a Canadian UPS Store address as the official Shopify and business address: keep it in Shopify settings and on supplier and Google forms. An address on the website is optional. A colleague's Delaware address only "appeased Shopify and Stripe", and a coach used a Canadian address without issues.
- [07:26] Upload speed: on competitor category pages, the default sort (recommended or best selling) shows best sellers first, so upload those first for each supplier. Example site used: wayfair.com.
- [16:11] Trust call script: ask what concerns them. Say you're an authorized retailer for the brand "since 2022" (your real year), that the site is hosted on Shopify and payment is 100% secure, and that they can buy on the website instead of giving a card over the phone. Optionally say they're speaking with the owner and give your direct email (name@yourstore.com).
- [36:53] Supplier invoices when Shopify payouts take 48 to 72 hours: pay with a business credit card so orders ship right away and earn points or miles, instead of waiting for the payout and shipping a couple of days later.
- [37:39] Example: about 90% Beta clicks and 10% Alpha clicks can still work. To move traffic to Alpha, check search terms in both campaigns under Reports and insights and add negatives so terms fall through to the right campaign.
- [44:52] Traffic channels tested but kept out of the blueprint, most recently TikTok: if a strategy works on 1 of 5 stores and can't be repeated on the other 4, Course A doesn't teach it.
- [58:52] Price-point research: include everything on the first results page, even Temu and AliExpress. If enough cheap listings drag the average down, add a qualifying keyword, for example camping tents to "family size camping tents".
- [59:44] Funnel pages or single-product funnel sites are not recommended for this model.
- [1:02:51] Google shut down a campaign for "over serve prevention due to similar segment removal". Course A's own setup: one remarketing campaign with ad groups for 2-, 7-, 14- and 30-day visitors, each excluding the shorter ones. Google sometimes says "these are similar, remove some", and Course A ignores it because they work. If a campaign is turned off: switch off duplicate remarketing campaigns, or recreate it with a bigger window (14 to 30 days, 30 to 45 days).
- [1:04:46] Impression share example: Alpha at 60% for one brand, goal 80%+. Max bid $2.80 but average CPC only $2.07, so raising bids didn't help. Cause: other suppliers take most of the daily budget (example $20/day) because they have more volume at current bids. Fix: exclude that supplier (C) from the main Alpha and Beta and build "Alpha C" and "Beta C" with only that supplier and their own budget. Course A posted a Loom bonus video on this in the announcement channel.
- [1:07:49] Shipping disruptions (storms, tornadoes, the 2024 Maryland bridge collapse): notify affected customers about delays; they are usually understanding.
- [1:09:20] Multiple stores under one Shopify login vs separate logins: either is fine. Ownership transfer to a buyer is done by changing the admin email.
- [1:10:06] Brands found on Houzz: Google each brand name, open the stores that rank, and if they are online-only, the brand is a valid supplier lead.
- [1:12:25] Warranty by length (90 days, 1, 3 and 5 years): create one product template per warranty ("supplier ABC 90 days", "supplier ABC 1 year"...), each based on the previous one, with a collapsible "Product warranty" row, then assign each product to its template.
- [1:19:30] The header discount (expiring urgency offer) and the email pop-up or exit-intent overlay discount should be the same discount.
- [1:23:17] First social posts at launch: no big announcement needed. Post "We're pleased to announce we're now an authorized retailer for [brand]" with a link to the collection, or a product with a link and the main product image.
- [1:24:02] Brands that also sell direct are acceptable if (1) they sell direct at MSRP and let retailers sell at MAP, or (2) they have e-commerce but it is clearly not their focus: they don't appear in Google Shopping ads for their own products and their product pages "look like they were built 10 years ago". You become the retailer that improves those pages.
- [1:26:17] Demo product images when no stock image or Canva AI works: as a last resort, use 2 to 3 product images from a China site, remove any logo markings in Canva, and replace them as soon as the first approval comes in.
- [1:34:10] Course A does not use Alibaba to source suppliers; Alibaba is only for private label.
- [1:35:46] Uploading an unapproved brand's products as demo products: Course A did this about a decade ago without problems, but others had suppliers ask "why are our products on your site? You're not authorized", so use the most generic products you can find.
- [1:36:35] Dynamic remarketing: the remarketing campaign is linked to the product feed, so ads show the products people viewed and clicks go to that product page through the feed's links. No manual URLs are needed.
- [1:39:41] "There's a problem with your feed" error on remarketing: check that the GA data sharing settings are all turned on, then delete and recreate the campaign. If it persists, use Google Ads live chat support.
- [1:43:04] Typical start for Course A's own new campaigns: about $30/day, while $10/day is "more than enough".
- [21:58] Same warehouse address used by several stores: if it really is a warehouse, the owner is most likely a company building multiple niche stores that stocks inventory, not a dropshipper.
- [11:42] Review dates help, because they show the store is established. Over time newer reviews accumulate, so old dates aren't a negative.
- [26:40] Products with 3 to 6 week lead times: after each order, email the customer to confirm they know the current lead time ("custom made / backordered, current lead time 3 to 6 weeks"), and only proceed once they confirm.
- [27:26] Suppliers outside the UK that ship to the UK in reasonable time are OK, but check the tax implications first (for example EU to UK).
- [24:22] Refunds of unwanted items go back to the supplier's warehouse, and you get refunded at wholesale. Ideally give the customer a store credit instead of cash back. You never resell returned products yourself.
- [1:23:17] (2024) Some suppliers accept an EIN alone for approval; others also want the sales tax certificate from your home state.

### April 2025 Coaching Call: Lieferanten, Shopify und Pricing (Course A) [L0255, 2025]

- [10:55] Suppliers that don't ship (about a decade ago, from brands that contacted the store): if it happens more than once, remove all their products. For a stuck order, refund the customer, apologise that the product is unavailable, and suggest similar products from better suppliers. Course A never needed attorneys or contracts for this.
- [15:54] Course A's reaction to the 2025 economy: sales up about 10% in Q1, so nothing changes and he is spending a little more on ads.
- [16:45] On-page SEO lift can't be predicted, but moving from page 2 (no traffic) to positions 3 to 5 on page 1, or from 3 or 4 to 1, is a huge lift.
- [33:53] Facebook and Google reps who call to "help with your account" usually give advice that loses money, and they call a lot.
- [46:16] Microsoft Ads after a 4-month pause (example: 784 search impressions vs 26,000 audience impressions): if the Google Alpha and Beta changed since, turn off the old Microsoft campaigns, re-import the current Google campaigns, and set budgets to about 20 to 25% of Google's.
- [57:10] SEO priority: lessons 6.4.0 to 6.4.7 in order. The 80/20 is the product page SEO lesson first, because product pages are easiest to rank and most likely to sell.
- [1:12:20] Split payment on one order (part card, part wire): create two draft orders that add up to the total (for example $500 and $500 for a $1,000 product) and send each to the customer.
- [1:22:35] Members of the 100K Club ($100k+ sales; some at $3M, $5M and $20M) built their businesses on paid ads, and over time got free traffic close to matching paid revenue. So do the module 6 SEO lessons sooner rather than later.
- [1:24:11] Course A's own organisation system (2025): Apple Notes checklists for today, next week and end of month. No other productivity tools.
- [1:27:24] Shipping text for one supplier: example product page line "This product ships in 2 to 3 days via freight. We'll email you with a tracking number". Change it per supplier if their ship times differ. A longer supplier return window (90 days vs the 30-day default) is a selling point, so show it on that supplier's product pages and as an exclusion on the general return policy page.
- [1:30:08] Smart SEO sitemap app asks for Google Search Console access: grant it, but first make sure you are logged into the Google account connected to the store's Search Console.
- [1:31:11] CSV warning: Course A once sorted one Excel column without moving the others, uploaded about 100 products with mismatched data, and sold items at wrong prices with wrong descriptions. Double- and triple-check bulk uploads.
- [1:32:49] Course A runs one promotion per month (module 6 monthly promotions).
- [1:43:21] Slow store: run GTmetrix (free account) and open the Waterfall tab to find the asset causing the delay (example: an element taking 6.2 seconds, likely an embedded video), then remove it.
- [1:44:10] A PayPal payment method "pending review" in Merchant Center clears by itself once Google's bots confirm PayPal is available at checkout.
- [1:51:47] Course A's own price match policy: if a customer finds a lower price elsewhere within 6 months of purchase, the store refunds the difference. Shortening it to, for example, 30 days is allowed.
- [1:59:49] While the store is unlocked for suppliers with demo products (about a few days to a week), optionally disable payment methods or leave shipping unconfigured so nobody can check out.
- [2:01:32] Non-MAP supplier: if competitors on Google Shopping sell at about 5% margin, list at 2x wholesale and use free traffic only. If competitors keep a good markup, list at the same prices and include the products in Google Shopping.
